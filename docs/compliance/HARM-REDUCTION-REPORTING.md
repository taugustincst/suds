# Harm-reduction reporting (California)

What SUDS produces for the reports a California harm-reduction or prevention programme owes, where each figure comes from, and **what has not been verified** against the funder's current template. Nothing here is an official form: each file says so, and each must be checked against the funder's current instructions before it is submitted.

All three reports are aggregate. None contains names, client codes, dates of birth or record ids, and none is a disclosure under 42 CFR Part 2; running or exporting any of them is recorded in the audit log.

## 1. Funder report (Reports → Funder report)

`GET /api/reports/funder` (reports:read), `GET /api/reports/funder/export?format=xlsx|csv` (also export:read).

| Figure | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Unduplicated clients served | Clients (not deleted, within the caller's caseload) with a visit or a call in the period; with a fund chosen, only visits charged to it. Each person once. |
| Demographic breakdowns | Those people's gender, language, housing, insurance, race codes (a person may report several) and ethnicity. |
| By funding source | Visits in the period grouped by `interventions.funding_source_id`, with an explicit **No funding source** row; staff time from `time_entries` in the period: *approved* minutes and *logged, not yet approved* (draft or submitted) minutes. With a fund chosen, the table, the staff hours on the page and on the Summary sheet, and the attribution figures are that fund's alone (visits charged to no fund are not in a report about one fund). In a publication release every fund with fewer people than the threshold is in one row, *Other funds (n combined)* (how many funds, their staff hours; people and services `withheld`), since 1.14.0 (docs/HIPAA.md, *Small funds, combined*). |
| Overdose & naloxone | `overdose_events` and the naloxone kits and test strips on visits. *Who gave the naloxone* counts reversals (naloxone used, the person survived), as the NDP log does. |

**Small-cell suppression and publication** (`purpose`, `counts`; the same for all three reports, `server/publication-release.js` and `server/sdc.js` for a publication release, `server/small-cells.js` for any other run; the design and its residual risks in [HIPAA.md, Small cells in aggregate reports](../HIPAA.md#small-cells-in-aggregate-reports)):

* **What a run is for** (`purpose`, in the response's `suppression.purpose`, `suppression.label` and `release`). With no `purpose` asked, a supervisor, an administrator (`reports:internal`) or finance (`reports:funder`, 1.14.0) gets the **submission**, with exact counts; read-only gets the **publication release** (for a period that can be one); a navigator's or clinician's run is **internal** (the whole programme from 1.16.0; its caseload for a person held to it). The page shows which, first and prominently (*Submission to your funder — not for publication*, *Internal — not for publication*, or the publication label below).
  * `submission` — the programme's own report to its funder (or to the NDP), and the default for a supervisor or an administrator: **exact counts** (`counts=suppressed` for small cells suppressed), labelled *Submission to your funder — not for publication*, filename `…-exact-counts`. Not for publication. This is how a funder gets its own fund's figures, and what a quarter-end grant report is.
  * `publication` — a release to publish or share beyond the funder, prepared on request while publication releases are switched on (Settings › Program › Modules, *Publication releases*, on by default; switched off, every publication run and file is refused with a message and submissions carry on — ADR-0009's 1.14.0 addendum) (**Prepare a publication release** on the funder report, *Kind of file → Publication release* beside the harm-reduction exports; `purpose=publication`). Only for the **whole programme** (no `funding_source_id`, no caseload scope) and **one standard period that has ended**: a calendar month, a quarter (starting January, April, July or October), or a year starting on one of those quarters (a July–June or October–September fiscal year, or a calendar year). Labelled *Publication release — small cells screened; review before sharing* (its files end `-publication-screened-review-before-sharing`); its files are exported only with `reviewed=1`, the person's confirmation that they reviewed the withheld and small figures (428 without it; the page asks for a tick box), which the audit log records with the release id as `report.publication.reviewed`. Asking for `purpose=publication` on any other run is refused (400), because two runs of different scope or period can be subtracted from each other to reveal a small group (the whole programme minus one fund, January–June minus January–May).
  * `internal` — for the programme's own use (`purpose=internal`; the default for a caseload-scoped run): marked *internal, not for publication* on screen, on the About sheet and in the filename. `release.not_publishable` says why a run could not be published.
* **Counting** (`counts`): exact by default for a submission by a role holding `reports:exact`; suppressed otherwise, and always for a publication release. **Every count of people** above 0 and below the threshold is reported as `<N`, in every table and headline figure. The threshold is the `small_cell_threshold` setting (default 11, from 2 to 50).
  * Counts of people: unduplicated people served and each demographic row; new admissions, people referred, admitted after a referral and on MAT; episodes opened, closed and open at the end, and each discharge reason; people per fund (including the *No funding source* row) and per settlement allowable use; overdose events (in total and per month), reversals (in total, per month and per NDP row), fatal and community-reported overdoses, and who gave the naloxone (in each reversal). An overdose or a reversal is an event that happened to one person, so it is counted as one.
  * Not counts of people, and exact unless they would give a count of people away: naloxone kits and doses distributed, fentanyl test strips, services (visits), staff hours and money. A fund's or a settlement use's services bound its people (everyone served was served at least once), so in a publication release they are hidden (`suppressed`) with a hidden people count. Doses used in reversals bound them too (each reversal records 1 to 20 doses), so in a publication release the doses used, in total and in each NDP month, are cells of the same audit and are hidden with the reversals or events they would reveal. A publication release lists every month of the period (the funder report's overdoses by month and the NDP log's reversal rows) and every "given by" and discharge-reason code, zero or not, in a fixed order; a table withheld whole prints no rows (docs/HIPAA.md "Which rows are listed").
  * **One release, audited as one.** In a publication release the funder report, the NDP log (by month) and the settlement report for the period are one release: read together, audited together, and each printing its part of the same result (`release.id` is the same on all three, and asking again gives the identical release). The audit models what a reader of all three learns: the true count behind every cell, every relationship between counts (breakdowns add up to their totals; subsets are at most them; race codes cover the total; a use's people lie between its largest fund's and the sum of its funds'; a month's reversals are at most its events; fatal plus reversed events at most all events; people at most services), a number as itself, `<N` as 1 to N−1 and `suppressed` as at least N. It then hides more cells, in a fixed structural order, until every `<N` cell, and every unprinted count of people that the printout lets be small (the people left over from a subset, the events in a month that were not reversals, and so on - decided from what would be printed, never from the true count), could still be both 1 and N−1 (or as near as the symbols alone allow), and every `suppressed` cell still ranges over at least half the threshold, checked exactly over the integer solutions. Because SUDS is open source, the pattern of what is hidden is checked too: every hidden cell must be shown protected by worlds that print the identical release through the same suppression. When that check cannot show a table protected, the table is **withheld** and the rest of the release is suppressed and checked again without it (more strictly: see HIPAA.md, *Degrade, don't refuse*); the release is **refused** only when even the headline (people served) cannot be shown safely, when the degraded release fails too, or when the audit reaches its budget (counted in solver work, so the same figures always give the same answer). A small programme at a small threshold often is refused; publish a longer period. What cannot be protected is withheld whole: `release.withheld` lists the tables, `release.withheld_reasons` each with its label and why (*Too few people to show it without giving someone away*, or *The automatic check could not confirm that its small counts are protected…*), and `withheld` the demographic breakdowns; the page lists them in the review step. A cover's complement (another race code hidden beside a small one) is chosen from the printout alone, which is what let most quarters of 60 to 100 people publish again (1.12.4 refused them). The headline is never hidden beside figures that bound it: new admissions and episodes opened are hidden with it. The total stays visible whenever hiding cells of its breakdowns is enough. The design, the solver and the rule's reasons: [HIPAA.md](../HIPAA.md#small-cells-in-aggregate-reports).
  * **Complementary suppression in other runs.** A run that is not a publication release protects each table with its own total (as 1.12.1 did): a `suppressed` cell is at least the threshold, `<N` lies between 1 and N−1, and tables that share a total are protected together until nothing hidden can be pinned to one value. It is not audited across reports; it is not for publication.
  * A median length of stay over fewer people than the threshold is shown as `suppressed`.
  * A total that is itself a small count of people is shown as `<N`. Zero is shown as 0.
* **Who may run what** (`server/auth.js` `reportRunAllowed`, checked in `server/routes/reports.js` before the report is built, for the funder report, the NDP log and the settlement report and their exports): a run that is not a publication release — `purpose=internal` or `submission`, suppressed or exact, including the internal default for a custom range, one fund or an unfinished period — needs `reports:internal` (supervisor, administrator), or `clients:read` and a run that counts only clients the user can open (a navigator's or clinician's own caseload for the funder report and the NDP log; the settlement report counts the whole programme, so for them only when caseloads are not restricted). Finance (`reports:funder`, 1.14.0) also runs the programme's own **submission** — exact aggregate counts, by fund and for any range — and nothing else (`purpose=internal` is refused); its runs hold no client-level data, like everyone's, and each is audited with the purpose, fund and period. Readonly runs **publication releases only** (403 otherwise, audited as `authz.denied`), so it cannot subtract an internal run from a release.
* `counts=exact`: exact counts, for a run that is not for publication (`purpose=submission` or `internal`); the default for a submission by a role that holds `reports:exact` (supervisor, administrator) or `reports:funder` (finance, for the submission only), and `counts=suppressed` turns it off. The Reports page offers *Kind of file* beside the harm-reduction exports (the submission, exact; the submission with small cells suppressed; or a publication release, for a range that can be one). `counts=exact` with `purpose=publication` is refused.
* The response's `suppression` (`mode`, `threshold`, `purpose`, `label`), `release` (`publishable`, `period`, `not_publishable`; for a publication release also `id`, `withheld` and `withheld_reasons`) and `counting_statement` say which was used (the page shows two plain lines and puts the full statement behind *Why some numbers are hidden*); each export repeats it on its About sheet (funder report CSV: the first rows), in its filename (`…-publication-suppressed`, `…-internal-suppressed` or `…-exact-counts`) and in the `X-SUDS-Report-Counts` and `X-SUDS-Report-Purpose` headers.
* **Whom it protects against.** Suppression protects a publication release against its recipients (funders, the public) who have no other access to SUDS. Staff with `reports:read` or `export:read` are insiders: the dashboard, the monthly trends report and de-identified row-level exports already tell them more than a suppressed cell hides, so against them the controls are permissions, role assignment and the audit log ([HIPAA.md](../HIPAA.md#small-cells-in-aggregate-reports)). As defence in depth, internal and submission runs of these three reports need `reports:internal` (or a caseload-scoped role counting only its own caseload); finance's `reports:funder` adds the submission runs only; read-only accounts run publication releases only. A caseload run of the funder report counts only the caseload's people, people per fund and overdose events (community-reported events, which have no client, are left out), and says so (`caseload_scope_note`, the About sheet's *Scope*).
* **Residual risk.** This is a conservative screen, not a guarantee and not a statistical expert determination (45 CFR 164.514(b)(1)); review a release before sharing it. The suppression pattern of any deterministic method can say more to someone who knows the method; how 1.12.4 checks it, and what that check does not prove, is in [HIPAA.md](../HIPAA.md#small-cells-in-aggregate-reports). The audit covers one release. Two publication releases for nested or overlapping standard periods (a quarter and its year) can still be subtracted from each other, as can the same period run again after late entries. Publish one standard period per funder cycle, once its data are complete. Internal and exact runs can be subtracted from a release by whoever may run them, which the reporting permissions govern.

**Funding attribution.** A visit is charged to the fund the worker chooses; a new visit form is pre-filled with the worker's default fund (`users.default_fund_id`) or the programme's (`default_fund_id` setting), and a visit posted without the field (a role not shown it, an API client) is charged to that default. The report's `attribution` block counts the services with no fund and links to them (`#/interventions?funding=none`); when the programme has no default fund it says so (`default_fund_set`) and links to where one is set (`settings_link`, Settings → Programme → Reporting). The first-run setup wizard asks for the programme's main funding source (optional): it is created for the current July–June fiscal year and made the default, so a new install's visits are not all *No funding source*.

## 2. Naloxone Distribution Project log (Reports → Harm-reduction reporting)

`GET /api/reports/naloxone-ndp` (reports:read), `GET /api/reports/naloxone-ndp/export?format=xlsx|csv` (also export:read). Small cells as in section 1: reversals per row are suppressed; a read-only account runs publication releases only, and finance the submission or a publication release. **Published** (a publication release, section 1), the log is **by month**: distribution by month, site type and recipient type, and reversals by month for all sites together, which are the funder report's reversals by month: the same cells of the same release (section 1). A month's reversals may be `withheld` when a month has so few events and reversals that both cannot be shown. The day-by-day log by site and by who gave the naloxone is for the programme's own submission to the NDP (`purpose=submission`) or internal use.

The DHCS Naloxone Distribution Project (NDP) supplies naloxone to organisations that report back on what they distributed and on the overdose reversals reported to them. SUDS already records both, so no new data entry was added:

| Column | Source in SUDS |
| --- | --- |
| Date | The day of the visit or event, in the programme's time zone. |
| Entry | *Distribution* (kits handed out) or *Reversal reported*. |
| Site type | The same coded *Location* list for both (field, community, shelter…): distribution, the visit's; reversal, the overdose form's *Where*. A place typed in before *Where* was a list is matched to a code whatever its case (*Shelter* → shelter), or else counted as *Other*; the record keeps its words. |
| Recipient type | *Community member (anonymous)* — community distribution with no client — or *Program participant* (a client on the caseload). |
| Naloxone product | The day-by-day log only (not the published log by month): the product of the naloxone item the visit handed out (4 mg, 8 mg or 3 mg nasal spray, 0.4 mg injectable vial, ampule or prefilled syringe, other; [SUPPLIES.md](../SUPPLIES.md)), or *Not recorded* for kits recorded as a count alone, as every visit before 1.14 was. A visit with two products is two rows. |
| Kits distributed | Sum of *Naloxone kits given* (`interventions.naloxone_kits`, since 1.14 the sum of the visit's naloxone items). |
| Naloxone doses distributed | Kits × the `naloxone_doses_per_kit` setting (default 2: a standard nasal-spray kit holds two doses). |
| Reversals reported | Overdose events (Overdose & naloxone form, including community reports with no client) where naloxone was used and the person survived. An event recorded as a *Reversal* had naloxone by definition: the form ticks *Naloxone was given* when Reversal is chosen, the server records it so, and one saved before that rule with the box unticked is counted too. |
| Doses used in reversals | `overdose_events.naloxone_doses`. |
| Naloxone given by | The overdose form's *Given by*. |

Rows are aggregated by day, site type, recipient type and product (distribution) or day, site type and who gave it (reversals); in a publication release, by month, site type and recipient type (above). A navigator's log covers the whole programme from 1.16.0 (a navigator held to their caseload: community distribution and their own caseload), like every other report.

**Unverified:** the column layout follows the NDP's published reporting fields *as SUDS understands them*. It is **not** the official NDP template and must be checked against the current NDP reporting template (and its reporting period and submission method) before it is used. In particular: whether the NDP wants per-event rows or period totals, its own site-type and recipient categories, and whether its product categories match the ones SUDS records (since 1.14, from the naloxone item a visit hands out).

### Syringe services summary (Supplies → Syringe services report; Reports → Harm-reduction reporting)

`GET /api/reports/ssp`, `GET /api/reports/ssp/export?format=xlsx|csv` (export:read) — [SUPPLIES.md](../SUPPLIES.md). For a
period: participants served (unduplicated clients with a contact), contacts (visits and anonymous outreach at which
supplies were handed out or sharps brought back), syringes distributed and returned (counted, or estimated from the
container's volume, shown separately) and the returns per syringe distributed, sharps containers, naloxone kits (by
product), fentanyl and xylazine test strips, the other supplies by item, and referrals made for the participants;
by month and by supply site. Participants, the people referred and the referrals are counts of people and are
suppressed as in section 1; supply and contact counts are exact. It is always the programme's own submission (or
internal), never a publication release (`purpose=publication` is refused), so it is run by those who may run such a
report (`reportRunAllowed`: supervisors and administrators, or a navigator or clinician for their own caseload and
anonymous contacts). Since 1.16.0 finance (`reports:funder`) runs it too, as the programme's own submission of the
whole programme (`purpose=submission`; an internal run is refused), aggregate only, from the funder report page's
link (`#/ssp`) when it has no Supplies page. Read-only accounts are refused. **Unverified:** the layout follows what a
CDPH-authorised SSP reports as SUDS understands it; check it against the programme's current reporting requirements.

## 3. Opioid settlement expenditure report (Reports → Harm-reduction reporting)

`GET /api/reports/opioid-settlement` (budget:read), `GET /api/reports/opioid-settlement/export?format=xlsx|csv` (also export:read). Small cells as in section 1: people per allowable use are suppressed. Published, they are cells of the period's release (section 1), audited with the funder report's total, its funds and their services; a use's services are hidden with its people when they would bound them. Amounts and kits are not counts of people. People are counted as in the funder report: a deleted client's visits are services, not a person served. Finance runs it as the programme's submission (`reports:funder`) or as a publication release; a navigator (who holds budget:read) as a publication release only; an internal run of it is for supervisors and administrators.

A **settlement fund** is a funding source whose type is *Opioid settlement*, or one given a settlement category. Each fund has an **allowable use** and a **California High Impact Abatement Activity** (`funding_sources.settlement_use`, `settlement_hiaa`; migration 38); an expenditure has its own (`expenditures.settlement_use`, `settlement_hiaa`) only when it differs from its fund's. `none` means "not one of these" (for example an administrative cost, or spending that is not a High Impact Abatement Activity).

| Output | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| By allowable use | Approved or reimbursed amount, pending amount and number of expenditures in the period, per Exhibit E category, plus *uncategorised*. Rejected spending is left out. |
| By HIAA | The same per High Impact Abatement Activity, with *none* and *uncategorised*. |
| HIAA share | Approved spending on High Impact Abatement Activities as a percentage of approved settlement spending. |
| Services | Visits charged to settlement funds, by the fund's allowable use: services, people, naloxone kits. |

The category lists are in `server/constants.js` (`SETTLEMENT_USES`, `SETTLEMENT_HIAA`), with their sources in a comment:

* **Allowable uses**: Exhibit E, *List of Opioid Remediation Uses*, of the national opioid settlement agreements — Schedule A *Core Strategies* (A–I) and Schedule B *Approved Uses* (A–L). Labels are abbreviated.
* **High Impact Abatement Activities**: the six activities in the California State-Subdivision Agreement, as summarised by DHCS.

**Unverified:** both lists, their wording and the High Impact Abatement share a participating subdivision must meet should be checked against the agreement that governs each fund (later settlements reuse Exhibit E, but a county's own reporting template may group categories differently). SUDS does not decide whether a cost is allowable; it groups what the programme recorded.

## 4. The settlement report for DHCS, or for your county (Reports → Harm-reduction reporting → For DHCS or your county)

`GET /api/reports/opioid-settlement/export?layout=dhcs|county&format=xlsx|csv` (budget:read and export:read; the same run rules as section 3). Both are the programme's own report to DHCS or its county: always the submission (exact unless `counts=suppressed`), never a publication release (`purpose=publication` with a layout is refused, 400).

**The DHCS settlement expenditure layout** (`layout=dhcs`, 1.14.0). California cities and counties report settlement spending to DHCS each state fiscal year (July to June) on the *California Opioid Settlement Expenditure Reporting Form*, an online form since SFY 2025-26, completed per activity or program. SUDS lays its settlement spending out one row per **activity** — a settlement fund and the Exhibit E category and HIAA its spending went to (an expenditure's own category, else its fund's) — in the order of the form's fields as SUDS understands them from DHCS's *Preview & Guide* and training materials (SUDS could not read the form itself when this was built: check each column against the current form, which is completed online; the file is for copying from).

| Column | Filled by SUDS? | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reporting period | yes | The report's range. |
| Settlement(s) the funds came from | **no — the programme writes it** | SUDS does not record which settlement (Distributors, Janssen, Teva, Allergan, the pharmacies…) a fund's money came from. |
| Fund (CA Subdivision Fund, CA Abatement Accounts Fund or Plaintiff Subdivision) | **no — the programme writes it** | Not recorded in SUDS. |
| Funding source in SUDS, grant or agreement number | yes | The fund's name and grant number. |
| Activity or program name, description of the activity | **no — the programme writes them** | DHCS asks for the activity in the programme's own words. |
| Exhibit E schedule and category | yes | `SETTLEMENT_USES` (section 3); *Uncategorised* until the fund or expenditure says which. |
| High Impact Abatement Activity | yes | `SETTLEMENT_HIAA`, or *Not a High Impact Abatement Activity*. |
| How the activity meets the HIAA (200 words at most) | **no — the programme writes it** | DHCS asks for the rationale. |
| Organization(s) that received the funds | yes | This programme (`org_name`). A county reporting several subrecipients combines their files. |
| Amount expended (approved or reimbursed), amount pending, expenditures | yes | As section 3. |
| Services under the activity, people served under it | yes | Visits charged to the fund in the period, and the distinct people, **on the row of the activity they fall under**: the fund's own Exhibit E category and HIAA (set on the funding source), since a visit is charged to a fund and not to one of its expenditures. A fund's other rows (spending given another category) carry its spending only, so the column adds up (1.16.0; it used to repeat the fund's whole count on every one of its rows). **A number of people of 10 or fewer is written "10 or fewer"**: DHCS asks that no count of 10 or less be reported. In a suppressed run people are also protected as in section 3. |
| Outcomes and narrative | **no — the programme writes it** | |

The Excel file's About sheet repeats which columns SUDS filled and which the programme must write.

**A county's own template** (`layout=county`). A county that passes settlement money to community organisations often has its own quarterly or annual form. Under *Set up the county template* (budget:manage: finance, supervisors, administrators; `PUT /api/reports/settlement-layout`, audited as `settings.settlement_layout`) the programme names the template and lists its columns in order, each filled with one of the DHCS layout's fields above, left **blank** for the programme to write, or **fixed text** (a contract number) — no code. The export then gives the same rows in the county's columns. Besides the DHCS layout's fields, a column can take (1.16.0) the **naloxone kits** and **fentanyl test strips** distributed on the activity's visits and the **naloxone reversals** charged to its fund (10 or fewer written "10 or fewer"). Its **rows** are the settlement activities, or (1.16.0) **one row per fund of another type** — a block grant (SABG), SOR or county general fund template — with the same fields, the settlement-only ones (Exhibit E, HIAA) left blank, and every active fund of that type listed. A county column SUDS does not hold (a county's own outcome measure) stays blank; the outcomes SUDS can count for settlement money are in section 5. One template is kept per programme. **Unverified:** SUDS has not been matched against any particular county's current form; the mapping is how a programme matches its own.

## 5. Settlement outcomes (sidebar → Settlement outcomes; 1.17.0)

`GET /api/reports/settlement-outcomes` and `…/export?format=xlsx|csv` (reports:read and budget:read, and reports:funder or reports:internal: finance, supervisors and administrators; the file also needs export:read). For each opioid settlement fund (a fund of source type *Opioid settlement*, or one given a settlement category) — a county's, a city's and the state's share each on its own — what it spent and what the programme recorded of the work charged to it, by Exhibit E category, by fund and by month, with the cost per outcome where that means something. It is **the programme's own view**: SUDS reports what the programme records. It is not an official state reporting system and not a state template (section 4 is the DHCS layout); nothing is sent anywhere, and the file is made when the signed-in person downloads it (audited as `report.settlement_outcomes` and `report.settlement_outcomes.export`, with the period, the counting and the format — never a figure about a person).

**What counts for a fund.** Visits, overdose events, episodes of care and time entries each name the fund they are charged to; a referral counts for a fund when the person referred was served under it in the period. Spending is approved or reimbursed expenditure (pending shown apart); the cost per outcome divides the spending under the **fund's own** category by the outcome, since the work is charged to the fund, not to one of its expenditures (spending the fund recorded under another category is shown beside it).

**Which outcomes, per category** (`server/settlement-outcome-map.js`, one place, with `test/settlement-outcome-map.test.js`):

| Exhibit E category (or the HIAA, when no category is set) | Outcomes shown |
| --- | --- |
| Core A (naloxone); HIAA 6 | Naloxone kits distributed, overdose reversals reported (naloxone used, the person survived), contacts |
| Core H (syringe services), Approved H (harm reduction); HIAA 4 | Contacts, naloxone kits, fentanyl test strips, syringes, reversals reported, people served |
| Core B, C, D, F; Approved A, D, E (treatment); HIAA 1, 2 | People served, treatment admissions (episodes opened), people linked to care (a referral admitted or completed), people linked to medication for OUD (a MAT referral admitted or completed) |
| Core E, Approved B, C (warm hand-offs, recovery, connections to care); HIAA 3 | People served, referrals made, people linked to care, people linked to MOUD |
| Core G, Approved F, G (prevention); HIAA 5 | Education and training sessions (visits recorded as *Education*), people trained (the distinct people at them), contacts |
| Approved I, K (first responders, training) | People trained, education sessions, staff training hours (approved time recorded as *Training*) |
| Core I, Approved J, L, and *not a remediation use* | None: planning, leadership, research and administration are shown as spending only, with a sentence saying so |

A cost per outcome is shown for kits, reversals, contacts, people served, linked and trained, admissions and sessions; not for strips, syringes, referrals made or hours.

**Small counts.** Every count of people (people served, linked, linked to MOUD, trained; referrals, reversals and admissions, each one person's) goes through the funder report's rules (`server/small-cells.js`) with its counting modes: on the page and in the file they are **suppressed by default** — under 11 shown as `<11`, and whatever would let one be worked out (a total, the other funds or categories, the other months) hidden as `suppressed` — for finance as for everyone. Each figure is protected across every place it is shown: all settlement funds together, each category and each fund (reversals and admissions, which are each one fund's, as a partition of the total; people, who may count under two funds, as subsets of it; people served also against the programme's people served, which the funder report prints), and each fund's months against its period. **No cost per outcome is shown beside a hidden count**: the money is exact, and the division would give the count away. Exact counts are there on request (`counts=exact`) for the roles the funder report lets run them — the programme's own submission (finance, supervisors, administrators) or an internal run (supervisors, administrators) — and never as a publication release (`purpose=publication` is refused). Money, kits, strips, syringes, contacts, sessions and hours are exact. No names, client codes, record ids or dates of service are in the page or the file.

**Unverified.** The mapping is SUDS's reading of what each Exhibit E category pays for; a county or the state may define the outcome of a category differently. Check what your agreement or county asks for before quoting these figures as the outcome of a grant.
