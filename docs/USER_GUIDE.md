# SUDS user guide (navigators, clinicians, supervisors)

## The three things to know
1. **Home** shows what needs attention today and where you left off — on any device.
2. **+ Log** (blue button; bottom-right on a phone) records a visit, call, text, note, to-do or time.
3. The **search box** at the top finds a client by last name, phone or code. Open a client to see their whole story.

Every page has a **?** that explains it in plain language. Records are never truly deleted, so you cannot break anything.

**Two words used everywhere.** A **visit** is any service you provide — face to face or by phone: outreach, a screening, a warm handoff, a naloxone kit, a ride (reports and exports call them *Visits*). A **to-do** is a follow-up or reminder, yours or one a visit or call created for you (the **To-dos** page, the client's *To-dos* tab, **+ To-do**).

## Your sidebar
The sidebar shows the pages your role uses every day. For navigators and clinicians that is Home, My clients, To-dos, Visits, Calls & texts, Notes, Supplies, Overdose & reversals, Referrals and the Resource directory; **More** at the bottom opens the rest you may use (Waitlist, Forms, My time, Reports, Privacy & Part 2, Import). Supervisors, finance and administrators also see the **Programme** section — Reports, Funder report, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2, Import and Settings. Nothing is taken away from anyone: a page that is not in your sidebar still opens from a link or its address if your role may use it, and client assistance (bus passes, IDs) is recorded on the client's *Assistance $* tab.

## Programme profile and modules
An administrator chooses what kind of programme this is in the setup wizard, and can change it at any time under **Settings → Programme → Programme profile & modules**:
* **Harm reduction & outreach** (the choice for a new install): outreach and visits, supplies, calls and texts, referrals, and grant reporting. The clinical modules are hidden.
* **Treatment-adjacent**: all of that plus the clinical modules — **care plan & problem list**, **assessments** (six-dimension ASAM-aligned assessments and scored screenings), **CalOMS Tx** state reporting, the **FHIR API** and the **county EHR hand-off**.

Each module can also be switched on (or off) by itself, whatever the profile — a harm-reduction programme that keeps a care plan switches on just that one. A module that is off leaves the menus, the client's tabs, the Overview and Reports, and SUDS refuses new records in it; anything recorded while it was on can still be read, and permissions do not change. A SUDS that was upgraded from an earlier version keeps everything it was using: it starts as treatment-adjacent if it already had any care plan, assessment, CalOMS or FHIR set-up, and as harm reduction otherwise. Devices that sync with the office show what the office shows.

## Phone, tablet and computer stay in sync
Use SUDS wherever you are: on the office computer, or in the browser on your phone or tablet at **https://suds.local** (office Wi-Fi). There is no app to install: open the address, sign in, and add it to your home screen once — iPhone: Safari **Share → Add to Home Screen**; Android: browser menu **⋮ → Install app**. The `/app` page (**https://suds.local/app**) shows these steps. Your clients, notes, reminders and even half-written note drafts are the same everywhere, because every device talks to the same office SUDS.

SUDS needs a connection to the office. Without one, a banner says so and nothing can be saved until you reconnect. If your programme has approved an offline copy for field work (local mode), your administrator will set it up with you; the office SUDS is always the master copy, and anything it refuses on sync is final.

## Signing in
Use your individual username. After 5 wrong passwords the account locks for 15 minutes. You are signed out automatically after 15 minutes without activity — a banner warns you one minute before. Supervisors and administrators must enroll an authenticator app (Profile → Multi-factor authentication → scan the QR code).

## Dashboard
Shows alerts (overdue to-dos, unsigned notes, imported notes waiting for review, clients with no visit in 30 days, consents expiring, open patient-rights requests and how many are past their 30-day deadline, active clients with no Part 2 notice on record, and — for supervisors — open privacy complaints and incidents nearing or past their notification deadline), your caseload sorted by risk and last contact, and 90-day activity charts. The budget figure (spent of budget) is shown only to the roles that approve spending — supervisors, finance and administrators. The same open-requests count appears on the Supervision page.

## Clients
* **Search** matches exact last name, "Last, First", phone number, date of birth (YYYY-MM-DD) or client code, plus the first few letters of a surname and (for names in the Latin alphabet) close misspellings. Names in any script work — Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese and so on — and accents do not matter: *Oster* finds *Øster*, *Lecki* finds *Łecki*, *Jose* finds *José*.
* **New client** — enter what you know; only first and last name are required. Set status *waitlist* if not yet enrolled.
* The client page has tabs: Overview, Timeline (everything in date order), Visits, Calls, Notes, Problems, Care plan, Assessments (clinicians and supervisors), Referrals, Forms, To-dos, Episodes, Consents & ROI, Requests, Time, Assistance $ (for roles that see the budget) and Care team. Problems, Care plan and Assessments appear when the programme uses those modules (see *Programme profile and modules*). See *Problem list, care plan and assessments* below.
* **On a phone** the client page keeps to what you need in the field: one **Add…** button opens Visit, Repeat last visit, Call, Text message, Note and To-do (on a computer they are separate buttons), and the tabs show Overview, Visits, Notes, To-dos and Consents, with every other section under **More**. A reminder you have already dismissed once (such as setting up two-step verification) comes back as a single line.
* **Requests** records patient-rights requests (access, amendment, restriction, accounting of disclosures), each due 30 days after it was received. Mark one *Fulfilled* or *Denied* when answered; **Edit** corrects its kind, dates or notes (or reopens it); **Delete** is only for a request recorded in error — a request you are refusing is marked *Denied* so it stays on file. Edits and deletions are in the audit log.
* **Safety flags** (e.g. "no home visits alone") appear as a red badge on every page for that client.
* **Status and episodes.** Starting an episode of care makes the client *active* (including someone taken off the waitlist). While an episode is open, *Closed* and *Deceased* cannot be chosen on the Edit form: discharge on the Episodes tab instead — that closes the episode, ends the care team and clears open to-dos. The discharge dialog has no default reason; choose one.
* **After a discharge**, a navigator whose only link to the client was their assignment loses access to the record the next day (the discharge ends every assignment). If you need to look back at a discharged client — to answer a records request, or when they return — ask a supervisor to assign you again, or to look it up for you. Supervisors and administrators see every record.
* **Duplicates.** Merge a duplicate from the Care team tab. The record merged away is kept, marked as merged, and an old link to it opens the record it was merged into. A record on **legal hold** cannot be merged, in either direction, until an administrator clears the hold.
* **Legal hold** can only be placed or cleared by an administrator (it usually comes from counsel). A supervisor who needs one placed should ask the administrator; the hold, its reason and who set it are in the audit log.
* **Contact details** are checked when saved: a date of birth cannot be in the future or before 1900, an email must look like one, and a phone number needs at least seven digits.

## Logging work
* **+ Visit** (or **+ Log → Visit**) — the core service record. Pick the type (outreach, SBIRT screening, warm handoff, naloxone distribution, post-overdose follow-up…), duration, location, outcome. Enter naloxone kits / fentanyl strips given — they roll up into reports and update the client's naloxone status. Set a follow-up date to create a to-do automatically. *Client* is optional for **outreach** and **naloxone distribution** (a kit handed to someone who gives no name counts in the funder report's community distribution); every other type needs the client. Leave *Also log as time entry* checked so your time sheet fills itself.
* **+ Call** — inbound/outbound, who, duration, outcome (reached, voicemail…), crisis flag, encrypted summary, follow-up.
* **Overdose & reversals** (sidebar) — record every overdose and naloxone reversal, including community ones with no client. Recording a **fatal** overdose for a client discharges them as deceased: after you confirm, their open episode is closed with the reason *deceased*, the care team ends and open to-dos are cancelled. If that was a mistake, change the outcome or **Delete** the event (in the event's dialog): the client goes back to the status they had, the episode is reopened, and the care team and to-dos are restored.
* **Time tracking** — non-client time (documentation, travel, meetings, training) is logged here; choose the funding source when the grant requires effort reporting.
* **To-dos** — follow-ups and reminders (**+ To-do** on a client, **+ Add a to-do** on the To-dos page); mark milestones (★) to show them on the client timeline.

## Problem list, care plan and assessments (CalAIM)
These are the **care plan & problem list** and **assessments** modules: they are shown when the programme is treatment-adjacent or an administrator has switched them on (see *Programme profile and modules*). They follow the CalAIM Behavioral Health Documentation Redesign: a current **problem list**, **progress notes** that say which problems they address, a **care coordination plan**, and assessment by the six **ASAM dimensions** (SUDS's six-dimension assessment is ASAM-aligned; it does not include the ASAM Criteria). How each requirement maps to SUDS, and what is out of scope, is in `docs/compliance/CALAIM.md`.

* **Overview → Clinical picture** sums it up: active problems, how many care plan goals are active and any review that is overdue (in red), the latest six-dimension assessment (the six ratings and the level of care), and each outcome measure's latest score with its trend. A PHQ-9 whose question 9 was answered above "Not at all" shows a red safety banner at the top. Each part appears only for roles that may see it.
* **Problems** — the client's current problems and needs, in plain words. **+ Problem** adds one, optionally with an **ICD-10-CM code** (typed in; SUDS checks the format — a letter, two characters, then optionally a dot and up to four more — and tidies it, so *f1120* becomes *F11.20*; there is no code look-up) and **social determinant (Z) codes** (open *Social determinants* to tick common Z55–Z65 codes such as Z59.02 Unsheltered homelessness, or type others). Set where it came from (self-report, assessment, referral) and when it started. Problems are never deleted: **Resolve** one (it is dated today) or make it *inactive*; **History** shows every change — who, when, and the old and new values. Everyone on the care team can keep the list; an administrator can read it.
* **Notes → Problems this note addresses.** The note form lists the client's active problems; tick the ones the service was about. The note shows them under *Addresses*, and the Problems tab counts the notes for each.
* **Care plan** — **+ Goal** records a goal *in the client's own words*, tied to a problem, with a target date and a **review date**. Once the review date passes, the goal (and the Overview) says *Review overdue*; **Reviewed** records that you went over it with the client today and sets the next review. **+ Step** adds what will be done, by whom (client, staff, family/support, another provider) and by when; tick *Also add it to the to-do list* to put a staff step on someone's to-do list, and marking the step **Done** closes that to-do. Mark a goal *Met*, *Partially met*, *Not met* or *Discontinued* rather than deleting it (delete is only for a goal entered in error, by whoever added it or a supervisor). **Print** produces a one-page plan with the active problems, goals and steps, and lines for signatures.
* **Assessments** (clinicians and supervisors) — **+ Six-dimension assessment** (ASAM-aligned) records a 0–4 risk rating for each of the six dimensions with what supports it, the **recommended** level of care, the level **referred to**, and — required when they differ — why (waitlist, client preference, not available…). SUDS records the ratings and the decision; use your programme's own licensed ASAM Criteria materials for the rating definitions. SUDS does not include the ASAM Criteria; "ASAM" is a trademark of the American Society of Addiction Medicine, and a programme that uses the Criteria needs its own licence from ASAM. The latest assessment sets the client's *ASAM level* field and is shown on the Overview; earlier ones stay in the history.
* **Outcome measures** — **+ PHQ-9**, **+ GAD-7**, **+ AUDIT-C** and **+ Wellbeing (0–10)** open the questionnaire. **+ DAST-10** appears only once an administrator has turned it on under **Settings → Screening instruments**, confirming that the programme holds the rights to use it (its licence covers non-commercial clinical use with credit); results already recorded stay visible if it is turned off again. The score and its band appear as soon as every question is answered and are saved with the answers (encrypted). For AUDIT-C, choose the cut-off (men 4+, women 3+; unspecified uses 3+). Give the same measure again over time: the tab and the Overview show the trend. Click a result to see the answers; **Delete** is for a questionnaire entered in error.
* **PHQ-9 question 9.** Any answer above "Not at all" raises a **safety alert** when the PHQ-9 is saved, whatever the total: an urgent to-do for today is put on your list, and the alert offers the client's signed safety plan (or **Write a safety plan now**, which opens a note in the *Safety plan* format). Follow your programme's crisis protocol; for imminent danger call 988 or 911.
* **Programme outcomes** are on **Reports → Outcome measures**: for each measure, how many clients were screened, how many were screened twice or more in the period, the mean first and last scores, and the number and percentage who improved (lower is better for PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C and DAST-10; higher for wellbeing). **Export (de-identified CSV)** gives one row per client and measure, keyed by a record id drawn at random for that file (not the client code), with the year rather than dates and no answers or names.
* Instruments not included: BAM, BARC-10 and other questionnaires whose licence for use in software could not be confirmed. A programme that holds a licence can set one up as a county form (**Forms**).
* SUDS does **not** do billing, claims, e-prescribing (eRx) or medication administration records (eMAR).

## Referrals and resources
The **Resource Directory** holds treatment providers, MAT clinics, shelters, harm reduction, legal aid, transport, etc. Keep *Last verified* current (entries older than 6 months are flagged). Create a **referral** from the client's Referrals tab, link the **consent** that authorizes sharing — it must name that provider (or its organisation) — then update the status as it moves: pending → contacted → accepted/waitlisted → scheduled → admitted → completed. Record barriers so the program can report on them.

**Provider pictures.** After adding a starter directory, **Download provider pictures** replaces each program's generated card with the picture from the provider's own website: on an office server it downloads them (the server needs internet access), on SUDS on this device it uses the copies the site was published with. When it finishes it says how many pictures it got — on screen and in a pop-up message — and, under "Why some had no picture", why the rest keep their generated card. On a phone or tablet that syncs with an office server the button is not shown: the office's pictures arrive with the next sync. You can always add your own photos on a program's page (**+ Add pictures**).

**Pictures on a program's page.** A program can have up to 12 pictures; the first is its main picture and the directory card's cover. There are four ways to add one, and all of them save the picture the same way (shrunk on your device first):
* **+ Add pictures** opens your computer's or phone's file window. That window belongs to the operating system and can open behind the browser; the card says when one has opened.
* **Drag** picture files from a folder or another window onto the Pictures card (it is outlined while you drag), or **paste** a copied picture (Ctrl+V / ⌘V) anywhere on the program's page outside a text box.
* **Add from a web address** takes the address of a picture (https:// only), with an optional caption. On an office server the server downloads it — or, given the address of a web page, the preview picture that page advertises — with the same safety checks as provider pictures: never an address on the office network, JPEG, PNG or WebP only, 2 MB at most. The audit log records the site's name, not the full address. On SUDS on this device, and on a phone or tablet copy, your browser fetches the picture itself, and most websites do not allow that: you then see *That site does not allow its pictures to be copied from a browser* — save the picture to the device and use **+ Add pictures**, or drag it onto the card. (On a device, a plain http:// address is accepted only for the site SUDS itself is served from, or for this computer.)

Do not add pictures of clients.

## Notes
* **Administrative / contact notes** — visible to the whole care team; use for contacts, coordination, logistics.
* **Clinical notes** — only clinicians and supervisors can write or read them; use SOAP/DAP/BIRP/GIRP formats (sections auto-build the narrative).
* Notes are saved as **drafts**. **Sign & lock** (re-enter your password) when complete. Signed notes cannot be edited or deleted; add an **addendum** for corrections or late entries.
* **Verify signature** on a signed note checks the note as it is stored now against what was signed: *Signature intact* means it is exactly as signed; *Changed after signing* means the stored note no longer matches — report it to your privacy officer. Click the signature hash to see it in full.
* Do not put names or other identifiers in intervention *summary* fields — they are not encrypted. Use notes for anything sensitive.

## Consents & ROI (42 CFR Part 2)
Your programme is a **42 CFR Part 2** programme: the client header says so, and the rules below are enforced — SUDS refuses what the rule does not allow and tells you why.

* **Give the patient notice.** When a client starts, give them the programme's notice of privacy practices (Privacy & Part 2 → Patient notice has the current version to print) and record **+ Notice given** on their Consents tab. Until you do, the Overview says *Not recorded* and Home counts them.
* **Record the consent.** **+ Consent** starts on the single consent for **treatment, payment and health care operations**, with the wording the rule allows. Fill in what the information covers and when it expires, who signed (the client, or a parent, guardian or representative — then their name), and tick each statement only if the signed form really contains it: the right to revoke in writing, the redisclosure statement, and what happens if the client refuses to sign. Use a separate consent for **SUD counseling notes** or for use in a **legal proceeding** — each of those covers nothing else. A general **ROI** is not a Part 2 consent. **Print** on a consent gives a PDF of everything recorded. A consent marked *Pre-2024 form* is still in force; renew it on the current form when you next see the client.
* **A consent covers only who it names.** A referral, a disclosure or a file under a consent is refused unless the consent names the recipient (case, spacing and punctuation do not matter; a referral is checked against the provider's name and its organisation). On the treatment, payment and operations consent, write the providers' names, e.g. *County Behavioral Health, Riverbend OTP and my other treating providers*: the class wording on its own names nobody SUDS can check. If a consent really does cover a recipient SUDS cannot match, a supervisor can override, with a written reason that is kept with the disclosure.
* **Record every disclosure.** Referrals record their own. For anything else shared, **+ Disclosure**: who, what, why, how and on what basis. When you share under a consent, SUDS shows the **§2.32 notice** — put it on the fax cover, letter or email. Tick *includes SUD counseling notes* or *for use in a legal proceeding* when that is what it is; SUDS then asks for the consent or court order that covers it.
* **Without consent.** A referral can rest only on the client's consent, a medical emergency (with a written reason), a court order, or a supervisor's justified override. On **+ Disclosure**: a disclosure to a qualified service organization, or for research or an audit, needs the agreement or approval with that organization on file (Privacy & Part 2 → Agreements); research, audits, reports of a crime on the premises and child-abuse reports are recorded by a supervisor, with a written reason for the last two.
* A consent marked *Cannot authorise a disclosure* is missing a required element (for example one that came from a device without them): record a new one.
* **Courts and subpoenas.** A subpoena alone never allows you to share a client's record. A supervisor records the **court order** on the Consents tab (Court orders); only an order with the required findings and notice can be relied on. Ask your supervisor and county counsel before responding to any legal request.
* **Agreed restrictions.** If a client's restriction request has been agreed (Requests tab), SUDS asks you to confirm you have checked it before anything is shared.
* **Revoke** a consent when the client withdraws it: open referrals that relied on it are flagged.

## Privacy & Part 2 (supervisors and administrators)
The **Privacy & Part 2** page (sidebar) has the programme-level controls; navigators and clinicians see the patient notice and the list of their clients who have not been given it.
* **Overview** — counts of what needs attention; an administrator sets whether this is a Part 2 programme and the size of identified export that opens a draft incident. Switching Part 2 off needs a written reason; it is audited, shown to administrators on Home until switched back on, and opens a draft incident for the privacy officer.
* **Agreements** — qualified service organization agreements, and the IRB or oversight approvals for research and audits, with every name the organization goes by. Disclosures on those bases may only go to an organization registered here. Supervisors and administrators add and end them; everyone who records disclosures can see them.
* **Patient notice** — the notice of privacy practices, ready to print. An administrator edits it (each save is a new version) once counsel has approved the wording.
* **Complaints** — log every privacy complaint, named or anonymous. Record whether the person was told they may also complain to HHS, check no adverse action followed, and close it with its resolution. Complaints are never deleted.
* **Incidents & breaches** — record anything that may have exposed client information the day it is discovered. Fill in the four-factor risk assessment, make the determination (a breach is presumed unless the assessment shows a low probability of compromise), link the affected clients and record each notice as it goes out (a client whose record is later purged stays listed by code, as breach documentation). The deadline (60 days from discovery) and whether HHS and the media must be told are worked out for you; Home warns two weeks before and when late. A failed audit-log check, a break-glass access you flag as a concern (Supervision → Access to review → *Flag a concern*) and a very large identified export open a draft here automatically.

## Budget
Finance staff see client codes on Budget and Time but not client records; the codes are not links for them, and opening a client link says *Not available for your role*.

Record client assistance (bus passes, IDs, motel nights, phone minutes…) against the correct funding source and budget line; attach the client so per-client spending is visible. A supervisor or finance staff approves; you cannot approve your own entries.

## Changing the choices on forms (administrators)
**Settings → Lists** holds the drop-down choices on documentation forms, grouped by form and named the way the forms name them: *What did you do?*, Location, Modality and Outcome on visits; *Who* and the call and text outcomes; referral *Status / What happened* and *If it did not happen, why*; the overdose form's *What happened* and *Given by*; time categories; note formats; primary substance; reasons for discharge. An administrator (the `settings:manage` permission) can, for each list:

* **Reword a choice.** Type the new wording and press Save (or Enter). Forms, lists, filters, reports and Excel/CSV exports all use it. Only the wording changes: records keep the same stored value, so old records and reports mean what they always did. Clear the box and save to go back to the built-in wording.
* **Reorder** with the ↑ and ↓ buttons.
* **Hide (retire) a choice** the programme does not use. It is no longer offered on new records; records that already have it still show it, and editing one keeps it (shown as *no longer offered*). **Show** brings it back.
* **Add a choice** of your own. SUDS makes its stored value from the wording, and never one that a built-in choice already uses. A choice you added can be reworded or hidden like any other.
* **Restore defaults**: the built-in wording and order, with every built-in choice offered again. Choices you added are hidden, not deleted, because records may use them.

Choices marked **Used by SUDS** drive a count or an automatic step — for example a call *reached* or a text *replied* counts as contact with the client, a *fatal* overdose or a *deceased* discharge marks the client deceased, outreach and naloxone distribution can be recorded without a client — so they can be reworded but not hidden. Referral statuses and the overdose *What happened* choices can be reworded and reordered but not added to, because each one means something to the consent check and the counts. Race and ethnicity, ASAM level, stage of change, consent types, patient-rights requests and funding types are national or legal code sets and are not editable; the page lists them and says why.

An administrator sees a small **Edit this list** link beside each of these drop-downs on the forms themselves, which opens that list. Staff see changes the next time they sign in or reload. The lists belong to the office SUDS: a device that syncs with it receives them and cannot change them; SUDS on this device (with no office) keeps its own.

**Funding sources** are at the bottom of the same tab for anyone who manages the budget (`budget:manage`: administrators, supervisors, finance — finance uses **Funding & spending**, which has the same controls): add one quickly (name, type, period and total award; budget lines and the rest are on Funding & spending), rename it, or deactivate it so it is no longer offered on new records (records already charged to it keep it). A **Manage** link beside every *Funding source* drop-down opens it.

## Reports and Excel
Choose a date range for program summaries and monthly trends. The dates are calendar days where the programme is (**Settings → Programme → Your programme → Organisation time zone**, else the server's `ORG_TIMEZONE`; SUDS on this device starts with the browser's time zone), so an evening visit on the last day of a quarter is in that quarter. In the funder report, *people served* is everyone with a visit or a call in the period (only visits charged to it, when a funding source is chosen), and every per-person count and breakdown on the page is counted within that same group. **Episodes of care** shows admissions in the period (and how many are still open), discharges in the period by reason, and the list of admissions; a client code opens the client only for roles that can see client records. **Export to Excel** (or CSV) is available on Reports, in three groups: *Funder & programme* (everything as one workbook — which also works in local mode — the funder report, staff time, resources and, for roles that see the budget, funding, budget lines and expenditures), *De-identified records* (clients, visits, calls, referrals, to-dos, consents, episodes, overdose events, client forms and disclosures) and *Records (identified)*; exports are also on the Clients, Resources, Visits, Calls, Time, Referrals and Budget pages. De-identified exports (the default) carry no names and no client codes: each row has a record id drawn at random for that file, dates are reduced to the year, and free text is left out. Supervisors can produce an identified workbook, which is recorded in the audit log.

## Funder report, funding attribution and harm-reduction reports
**Funder report.** Counts people, each once. Every count of people with fewer than the small-cell threshold (11; **Settings → Programme → Reporting**) shows as `<11` so nobody in a small group can be picked out — breakdown rows (race, gender, language…), people per fund, overdose events and reversals by month, discharge reasons, and a total that is itself small — and wherever a hidden figure could still be worked out from the other figures in the report, more is hidden and shows as `suppressed` (the total too, only when nothing else will do; a breakdown too small to show safely is withheld). Kits, test strips, services, hours and money are exact; naloxone doses are exact unless they would show how many reversals a hidden cell holds. The NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page count the same way. In a publication release every month of the period and every "given by" and discharge reason is listed, even when it is 0, rare typed-in values (a language only a few people speak) beyond the first ten are combined as *Other (combined)*, and a period whose figures cannot be checked in full is not published at all: publish each period once, never a period that overlaps or contains another you have published, and look over what is withheld before you share it. Small cells are screened automatically, which is not a guarantee: before a publication release's file downloads you tick **I have reviewed the withheld and small figures before sharing** (recorded in the audit log). A small programme's month is sometimes refused: SUDS checks that the pattern of what it hides says nothing more to someone who knows how SUDS works, and publishes a period only when it can show that; publish the quarter or the year instead.

**Publishing.** Only one kind of run is a **publication release**: all funding sources, for one calendar month, quarter or fiscal year (starting in January, April, July or October) that has **ended** — the buttons under *Periods you can publish* choose one. The banner then says *Publication release*, and the report and its files say *Publication release — small cells screened; review before sharing* (the filename ends `publication-screened-review-before-sharing`). Any other run — one funding source, a custom date range, a year to date, or a navigator's own caseload — says **Internal, not for publication**, and so does its file (the filename ends `internal-suppressed`): two reports of different scope or period can be subtracted from each other to reveal a small group, however carefully each one is suppressed. Publish **one standard period per funder cycle** (for example the fiscal year, once its data are complete): a quarter and the year that contains it can still be subtracted from each other. **Who can run what.** Supervisors and administrators can run any report: a custom range, one funding source, a period not yet ended (all *internal, not for publication*), and exact counts. Navigators and clinicians run it for their own caseload, which is always internal and always suppressed. **Finance and read-only accounts run publication releases only** — the page opens on the last quarter that has ended and offers only the *Periods you can publish*; a link to any other run says why it is refused. Any internal run could be subtracted from a release to reveal someone (August's release minus 1–30 August is whoever was served on the 31st), so internal runs stay with the roles that can already see client records or run the programme. Finance's money and staff hours are exact in a publication release and on **Funding & spending** for any period or fund. The same rule applies to the NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page (the settlement report counts the whole programme, so a navigator gets it only as a publication release too, and only while caseloads are not restricted). For the programme's **own submission to its funder** (a funder's own fund's figures), a supervisor or administrator can choose **Counts → Exact counts**: every cell is then the true number, a banner says so, and the exported file says *Exact counts … not for publication or sharing* on its About sheet (a CSV says it in its first rows; the filename ends `exact-counts`). **This report (Excel)** / **CSV** download the report as shown.

**Funding source on visits.** A new visit is pre-filled with the worker's default fund (**Settings → Users & roles → Edit → Default funding source**) or, if they have none, the programme's (**Settings → Programme → Reporting**); the worker can still choose another or none. A visit logged by someone who is not shown the field (a clinician) is charged to the same default. The funder report's **By funding source** table has a **No funding source** row, and a warning with a link to exactly those visits so each can be edited. Staff hours: **Approved staff hours** is what a county would invoice; **Logged, not yet approved** (draft or submitted time) is shown beside it, with a warning, so time waiting for approval does not read as zero.

**Harm-reduction reporting** (Reports, for the date range chosen). See [docs/compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md](compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md) for every field and what is not yet verified.
* **Naloxone distribution & reversal log (NDP-style)**, Excel or CSV: one row per day, site type and recipient type with kits and doses distributed (doses = kits × *Naloxone doses per kit*, 2 unless changed in Settings), and one row per day, site type and who gave it for reversals reported (overdose events where naloxone was used and the person survived). Kits come from *Naloxone kits given* on visits, including community distribution with no client; the site type is the visit's *Location*. No names or client codes. The layout follows the NDP's reporting fields as SUDS understands them and **is not the official NDP template**: check it against the current template before submitting.
* **Opioid settlement expenditures**, Excel or CSV (budget roles): spending from opioid settlement funds grouped by allowable use (the settlements' Exhibit E core strategies and approved uses) and California High Impact Abatement Activity, with the share that went to High Impact Abatement Activities, pending spending shown apart, and services charged to those funds. Set the category on the fund (**Funding & spending → Edit**), and on an expenditure only when it differs. The category lists need verification against each fund's agreement.

## State reporting (CalOMS Tx) and the county EHR hand-off
**Reports → State reporting** (shown when the programme uses the CalOMS Tx or county EHR hand-off module). A treatment program that must report CalOMS Tx has an administrator turn it on there (tick *This program reports CalOMS Tx* and enter the program's CalOMS provider ID). From then on, **Start an episode** on a client's Episodes tab asks the CalOMS admission questions, and **Discharge** asks the CalOMS discharge status and date of last service (and the past-30-day questions, unless the discharge is administrative). The **CalOMS records** button on each episode shows what it has, any problems, and what is still needed — for example an admission record for an episode opened at intake, or an annual update on each anniversary of admission. Prevention and navigation programs leave it off and are never asked.

The **Validation report** lists every problem in the period by client code and field (fatal errors first); fix them from the client's Episodes tab. A supervisor can **Download preview** — a zip of CSV files plus the monthly provider activity report, marked *PREVIEW - NOT FOR SUBMISSION*, with no names or dates of birth — to check it. When the records are ready, **Produce submission file**: that is the disclosure, so each client in it gets a *State reporting (CalOMS)* entry in their accounting of disclosures and its records show as sent, and the file downloads. Send that file, unchanged, through the county's CalOMS process; it is listed under *Submissions produced* (with its SHA-256) and can be downloaded again for 90 days. Records that still have fatal errors are held back. The layout is not yet verified against the current DHCS data dictionary: read `docs/compliance/CALOMS.md` before the first submission.

**SUDS does not submit Drug Medi-Cal claims.** Where a service must be billed, the **County EHR hand-off** on the same page produces the encounters (one row per client, per day, per kind of service and worker, with minutes, place and funding source) for entry into the county EHR. It includes names and Medi-Cal IDs, so it needs a recipient, a purpose and a lawful basis; with the consent basis, clients with no consent on file naming that recipient are left out and listed. It is never made on an "other" basis: a supervisor's justified override covers one client's disclosure, not a whole file. Identified exports on Reports work the same way; an *internal* export may only go to this program or its staff, and a QSOA, research or audit export needs the agreement or approval with the recipient on file. See `docs/SCOPE.md`.

## Importing spreadsheets
Import → *Import from Excel or CSV*. Choose what you are importing (clients, resources, visits, calls, time, to-dos, expenditures), download the template or upload the spreadsheet you already keep. SUDS matches your column names automatically (you can adjust them), checks every row, tells you exactly what is wrong with any row, flags people who already exist, and only saves when you click Import. For visits, calls and other client records, refer to the client by code (C26-0012) or "Last, First".
