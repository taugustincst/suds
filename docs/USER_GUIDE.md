# SUDS user guide (navigators, clinicians, supervisors)

New to SUDS, or want a page to print? [QUICK-START-WORKERS.md](QUICK-START-WORKERS.md) has one-screen task cards
in plain language: log a contact, street outreach with or without signal, to-dos, notes, referrals, a field phone.

## The three things to know
1. **Home** shows what needs attention today and where you left off — on any device.
2. **+ Log** (blue button; bottom-right on a phone) logs a visit, an overdose or reversal, a call, a text, a note, a to-do, a referral or time.
3. The **search box** at the top finds a client by last name, phone or code. Open a client to see their whole story.

Every page has a **?** that explains it in plain language. Records are never truly deleted, so you cannot break anything. The first time you sign in, a **Welcome to SUDS** card on Home (at the top on a computer, below today's to-dos on a phone) says the same in a few lines; **Got it** or its **×** puts it away for good, on every device you use, and **Help** at the foot of the menu brings it back. It never stands in front of the page: you can log a visit straight away.

**Two words used everywhere.** A **visit** is any service you provide — face to face or by phone: outreach, a screening, a warm handoff, a naloxone kit, a ride (reports and exports call them *Visits*). A **to-do** is a follow-up or reminder, yours or one a visit or call created for you (the **To-dos** page, the client's *To-dos* tab, **+ To-do**, and **To-dos for today** on Home). The buttons say the same everywhere: **Log a visit** and **Make a referral**.

## Your sidebar
The sidebar shows the pages your role uses every day, most used first. For navigators and clinicians (peer navigators included: they use the navigator role) that is Home, Clients, Waitlist, To-dos, Visits, Calls & texts, Notes, Supplies, **Street outreach**, Overdose & reversals, Referrals and the Resource directory; **More** at the bottom opens the rest you may use (Forms, My time, Prevention, Reports, the Funder report — an internal run, of the whole program (of your own caseload if an administrator holds you to it) — State reporting and SUPRT-A when your program uses them, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2, Import). The menu follows your program's profile (see *Program profile and modules*): in a **harm-reduction** program the Waitlist is under More; in a **Part 2 compliance module** program the harm-reduction pages (Street outreach, Supplies, Overdose & reversals, My time, the Funder report, Funding & spending) are not in it and Privacy & Part 2 leads. **On a phone** (the ☰ menu) a navigator's Notes is under More as well, so the menu is twelve entries at most — your unfinished notes are on Home under *Continue where you left off*, and the unsigned-notes link on Home opens them. A clinician (anyone who writes clinical notes) keeps Notes in the phone menu and finds Supplies under More instead (from 1.23.1). **Supervisors** see Home, then **Supervision** (the work waiting on them), then Clients, Waitlist, To-dos, Visits, Calls & texts, Notes, Referrals and **Settings** (moving a caseload and the audit log); **More** holds the rest they may use: Supplies, Overdose & reversals, Forms, My time, the Resource directory, Reports, the Funder report, State reporting and SUPRT-A when the program uses them, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2 and Import. Finance and administrators also see the **Program** section — Reports, Funder report, State reporting (CalOMS Tx and the county EHR hand-off) and SUPRT-A when those modules are on, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2, Import and Settings. On a phone the top bar's **Clients** opens the client list. Nothing is taken away from anyone: every page your role may use is in your menu, in the list or under More, and still opens from a link or its address; client assistance (bus passes, IDs) is recorded on the client's *Assistance $* tab.

## Program profile and modules
An administrator chooses what kind of program this is in the setup wizard, and can change it at any time under **Settings → Program → Program profile & modules**:
* **Harm reduction & outreach** (the choice for a new install): outreach and visits, supplies, calls and texts, referrals, and grant reporting. The clinical modules are hidden.
* **Treatment-adjacent**: all of that plus the clinical modules — **care plan & problem list**, **assessments** (six-dimension ASAM-aligned assessments and scored screenings), **CalOMS Tx** state reporting, the **FHIR API** and the **county EHR hand-off**.
* **Part 2 compliance module (beside an EHR)** (1.17.0): for a treatment program whose EHR stays the clinical record, SUDS does the 42 CFR Part 2 work the EHR does not. The care plan, assessments, CalOMS and the EHR hand-off start off (each can be switched on); the FHIR API starts on, so the EHR can read consents and their provenance. Supplies, Street outreach, Overdose & reversals, My time, Funding & spending, Settlement outcomes and the Funder report leave the sidebar (their addresses still work for roles that may open them), and **Privacy & Part 2** leads it, opening on the **Part 2 layer** tab: live, expiring and pre-2024 consents and active clients without one, disclosures by source, the patient notice, counseling notes, patient requests and their deadlines, breaches and complaints, secure referral links and the EHR integration, as counts (your own caseload's, if you are held to it). Import offers **Patients from your EHR** and **Encounters from your EHR**: upload the EHR's FHIR R4 export (a Bundle or Bulk Data NDJSON) and it goes through the same preview, checks and duplicate matching as a spreadsheet; SUDS never connects to the EHR itself. Every Part 2 control is the same in every profile; changing the profile deletes nothing. See `docs/integration/EHR-PART2-LAYER.md`.

Each module can also be switched on (or off) by itself, whatever the profile — a harm-reduction program that keeps a care plan switches on just that one. A module that is off leaves the menus, the client's tabs, the Overview and Reports, and SUDS refuses new records in it; anything recorded while it was on can still be read, and permissions do not change. A SUDS that was upgraded from an earlier version keeps everything it was using: it starts as treatment-adjacent if it already had any care plan, assessment, CalOMS or FHIR set-up, and as harm reduction otherwise. Devices that sync with the office show what the office shows.

## Phone, tablet and computer stay in sync
Use SUDS wherever you are: on the office computer, or in the browser on your phone or tablet at **https://suds.local** (office Wi-Fi). There is no app to install: open the address, sign in, and add it to your home screen once — iPhone: Safari **Share → Add to Home Screen**; Android: browser menu **⋮ → Install app**. The `/app` page (**https://suds.local/app**) shows these steps. Your clients, notes, reminders and even half-written note drafts are the same everywhere, because every device talks to the same office SUDS.

SUDS needs a connection to the office. Without one, a banner says so and nothing can be saved until you reconnect, except a street-outreach contact that names nobody, which waits on the phone and is sent when you are back online (released in 1.23.0; *Street outreach*). The banner is one short line (*Offline: a contact that names nobody is kept on this phone.*, from 1.23.1) and links, as **Working offline**, to **Set up this phone for the field**. A page that needs the office (Clients, To-dos…) says *You're offline* with a link to **Street outreach**, which still works; reloading the page with no signal keeps you signed in on that tab and shows the same, and everything is checked with the office again when the signal is back (released in 1.23.1). If your program has approved an offline copy for field work (local mode), your administrator will set it up with you; the office SUDS is always the master copy, and anything it refuses on sync is final.

## Signing in
Use your individual username. After 5 wrong passwords the account locks for 15 minutes. You are signed out automatically after 15 minutes without activity — a banner warns you one minute before. Supervisors and administrators must enroll an authenticator app (Profile → Multi-factor authentication → scan the QR code).

**Sign in with your fingerprint** (office SUDS only; released in 1.19.0). On a phone or computer with a fingerprint reader, face recognition or a screen lock, add a **passkey** under **My profile → Fingerprint sign-in → Add a passkey on this device** (your password again, and your authenticator code if you use one; then your device asks for your finger). From then on the sign-in page's **Sign in with fingerprint** signs you in without typing anything, and the username field may offer the passkey as a suggestion. A passkey counts as two-step verification: if your role requires it, the passkey is enough, and signing in with your password then finishes with your fingerprint. Your device may accept its PIN or screen lock instead of your finger: that is the device's choice. **SUDS never receives or stores your fingerprint** — your device checks it and SUDS keeps only a public key. Name each passkey so you can tell your devices apart, and remove one you no longer use (lost phone? ask an administrator to revoke it under Users). At most ten per person. See [FINGERPRINT.md](FINGERPRINT.md).

**Security & procurement** (released in 1.24.0). The sign-in page's footer, beside *Accessibility*, and the foot of the menu, beside *Help*, link to a page for organizations evaluating SUDS: what SUDS is, the two ways to run it, and links to the buyer guides, the security questionnaire, the threat model, the penetration-test scope (no test has been commissioned yet), the accessibility conformance report, the BAA/QSOA and other contract templates and the 90-day pilot kit, with the maintainer's contact, pricing and service levels where they have been published. It needs no account. An administrator of an office server publishes the contact details under **Settings › Program › Security & procurement page**; anything left blank reads *Not yet published by the maintainer*.

**Forgot your password or locked out?** On the office SUDS, ask your supervisor or a SUDS administrator to reset it (Settings → Users). If the administrator is the one locked out, whoever runs the SUDS server resets that account on the server itself (`npm run reset-admin -- <username>`, docs/INSTALL.md).

**On SUDS on this device** the records are encrypted with your password, so nobody can simply reset it for you. When the device is set up, SUDS shows a **recovery code** once: print it, or save it as a file straight to another device (a USB stick or another computer, not the device itself), keep it somewhere safe away from the device (whoever has it can open every record on the device, like a key), and tick *I have saved my recovery code*. The sign-in page's **Can't sign in?** section then offers three ways back in:

* **Use your recovery code** — type the code and a new password. You are signed in with every record kept, and SUDS shows a new code to save instead (the one you used stops working). Only the person who manages the device gets back in this way.
* **Restore from a backup** — puts a backup file back, with the accounts and passwords it had. Anything recorded since the backup is lost.
* **Start over on this device** — erases every record in this browser. Nothing is kept.

The person who manages the device can make a new recovery code at any time under **This device → Recovery code** (it asks for their password first; the old code stops working). If that person's account is deactivated, their code stops working at once, and the administrator who deactivated it is asked to make a new one. A device set up before this was added has no code: Home asks its manager to make one until they do.

**Scheduled backups on SUDS on this device** (released in 1.24.0). Your records exist only in this browser, so back them up off the device. The person who manages the device opens **This device → Keep your records safe → Set up scheduled backups**, types a backup passphrase twice and chooses how often (**every day**, **every 3 days** or **every week**). SUDS keeps a key made from the passphrase on the device, locked with your password like the records, so it never asks for the passphrase to make a backup; you need the passphrase to restore or check one, and nobody can recover it for you. Then:

* **On a computer with Chrome or Edge**, choose **Choose a folder** once (a USB stick, or a folder your county backs up). When a backup is due, SUDS saves it there by itself: when you sign in, and when you leave SUDS after a day's work. It keeps the newest backups (7, or the number you choose) and removes older SUDS backups from that folder; nothing else in the folder is touched. If the browser has been restarted it may ask again: Home shows **Back up to “your folder” now** — one click.
* **On Safari, Firefox, an iPhone or an iPad** the browser cannot save to a folder by itself. When a backup is due, Home shows **Download the backup**: one click, no passphrase. Move the file off the device (a USB stick, a county drive).

**Last backup** on This device says when and where the last one went, and **Next backup** when the next is due. **Back up now** makes one at any time. **Check a backup** opens the newest backup with its passphrase, as a restore would, and tells you whether it can be restored (nothing on the device changes); run it now and then, so you know the passphrase still opens your backups. Without scheduled backups, Home still reminds you weekly to **Download a backup**.

## Dashboard
**On a phone, Home starts with today's work.** First **To-dos for today** — what is due today and anything overdue, follow-ups included, overdue first and at most five, with **N more due to-dos** opening the To-dos page when there are more (from 1.23.1); tick a to-do's box to mark it done (the message offers **Undo**, which reopens it as it was, and, from 1.23.3, **View in Done**, which opens the To-dos page showing Done with it there — from 1.23.4 first in the list, marked *Just done*; the same message follows ticking a to-do off on the To-dos page, which keeps its own *Assigned to me* choice), or tap its title to open it, or the call, visit or referral it came from (from 1.23.2: tapping the title used to mark it done); when nothing is due, **+ Add a reminder** — then one row of links to what is waiting on you (unsigned notes, clients not contacted in 30 days, consents expiring), then **Continue where you left off**; the welcome card, your clients who need a check-in, and the program-wide figures come after. The program-wide cards (*at a glance*, the visits chart, the team's hand-offs, consents expiring) **fold**: press a card's heading to open or close it. On a phone they start folded; on a computer they start open. SUDS remembers what you open or close, for you, on every device. The **Update this page every 90 seconds** switch is at the foot of Home on a phone (in the heading on a computer). Which layout Home has follows the screen's width (up to 640 px, the phone's): from 1.23.3 it changes when the width does — a phone turned, a window resized, or a phone browser still sizing its window on a first sign-in, which used to leave *To-dos for today* below the first screen. If the keyboard focus was on Home's heading when it is laid out again, it stays on the new heading (from 1.23.4).

Shows alerts (for supervisors and administrators, the team's overdue to-dos; your own are counted on the 🔔 bell in the header and listed under **To-dos for today**; unsigned notes, imported notes waiting for review, clients with no visit in 30 days, consents expiring, open client rights requests (access, amendment, restriction, an accounting of disclosures) and how many are past their 30-day deadline, active clients with no Part 2 notice on record, and — for supervisors — open privacy complaints and incidents nearing or past their notification deadline), your caseload sorted by risk and last contact, and 90-day activity charts. The visits and naloxone kits counted there include outreach and community distribution recorded with no client, and kits handed out on any kind of visit (the **Naloxone kits given** tile opens the visits that handed some out): everyone's figures are the whole program's, and agree with the funder report for the same period, except for a navigator or clinician an administrator holds to their caseload, whose figures are their caseload's visits and the anonymous outreach they logged themselves (the activity card is headed *What the team has been doing* for anyone who sees every client — from 1.16.0 navigators and clinicians too —, *What you have been doing* for someone held to their caseload, and *The program's visits* for finance and read-only). A navigator's or clinician's to-do counts on Home stay their own. The budget figure (spent of budget) is shown only to the roles that approve spending — supervisors, finance and administrators. The same open-requests count appears on the Supervision page.

A **read-only account** (one that can look but not record anything) has a Home that starts with **Your reports** — the Funder report, Reports and the Resource directory — above the program's figures, and no *To-dos for today* or *Continue where you left off* card: it keeps no to-dos and opens no client records. Which cards Home shows follows the permissions an account actually has, including any an administrator has granted or denied it individually.

For **finance and read-only accounts**, Home and Reports show a count of people from 1 to 10 as **<11** (the same small-cell threshold as the funder report; read-only runs it as publication releases only, finance also as the program's submission to its funder); visits, calls, naloxone kits, hours and money stay exact. **Visits by worker** — a named staff member's activity — is shown only to supervisors and administrators. On a device copy there is no "also signed in on another computer" line: its sign-ins are all on that device.

**Your first day.** The welcome card on Home (until you press *Got it*; **Help** at the foot of the menu brings it back) holds three first steps for your role — for a navigator: log a visit, find a client, check your to-dos; a clinician: open the client list (their caseload, for a clinician held to it), write a clinical note, look over unsigned notes; a supervisor: Supervision, the caseload, the funder report; finance: funds, time to approve, the funder report; read-only: the funder report, reports, the resource directory. Each has the button that does it; using it, or ticking its box, marks it done. Someone who has already logged a visit or written a note before today does not get the list. An administrator has **Finish setting up** instead.

**The 🔔 bell** counts your to-dos due within the hour or overdue. Press it to see them, with when the list was last updated and **Refresh**. It asks again every minute, a moment after you save anything, and when you come back to the window.

**When someone not on the care team changes your client's record.** Anyone who can see a client may update their record. When the person who does is not on the client's care team, the client's primary worker gets a notice in their to-dos (and on the bell): who changed which fields. It opens as a card to read, not a to-do form, with **View client** and, for the primary worker it was sent to, **Mark as seen**. Anyone else who opens it (a supervisor, the editor) sees who it was sent to and whether they have seen it, and cannot mark it seen for them; only the primary worker's Overview says *changes to review*. The client's **Edit** form says so to the person editing, before they save. From 1.17.0 SUDS also keeps what the fields held before and after each change: the notice's **See what changed** opens the record's **History** at that change.

**A client record's History (1.17.0).** Under **More → History** on a client's record: every change to the client's own details, newest first — who made it, when, whether it came from a device's sync or a merge, and each changed field's value before and after. It is for the client's care team (anyone with an active assignment on the case) and for supervisors and administrators; someone else who can open the record sees it as it is now, not its earlier values. The primary worker, a supervisor or an administrator can **Put this change back**: the fields return to what they held before that change, as long as nobody has changed them again since (if they have, SUDS names the fields and you put back the later change first, or edit the record). Putting a change back is itself a new change in the History, and the primary worker is told as for any edit; nothing in the History is ever changed or removed. Changes made before 1.17.0 are not in it: SUDS did not keep earlier values then. On a phone or laptop that syncs with the office, the History is at the office (the device keeps no earlier values); a change made on the device appears there once it syncs. With SUDS on this device, the History is kept on the device. A change a discharge, an overdose event or a visit makes to the record (its status, overdose history, naloxone) is recorded on that record, not in the History.

**Finish setting up** (administrators) lists what a new office server still needs: the hardening steps below, the key backup, staff, the consent form, the resource directory, funding sources — and, once there are funds, **a default fund** (Settings › Program › Reporting) if none is set, and **each opioid settlement fund's category** when the settlement report would list its spending as *Uncategorised*. It also offers **Save a usual consent naming your referral partners**: the program's usual consent, which workers fill a new consent in from in one step. You tick the partners it names from the resource directory (nothing is ticked for you, and crisis lines are not offered: they are numbers a client is given, not organisations that receive records); they are added after the consent's wording, and are ticked again when you reopen it. And **Add your supplies**, while the Supplies cupboard is empty: one click adds *Naloxone kit* and *Fentanyl test strips* (with nothing on hand yet; record a stock-take under Supplies). Visits take kits and strips off those two items only once they exist; until then the visit form says so before it is saved (and offers a supervisor or administrator the same one click), and Supplies offers to add them.

**Hardening steps** (administrators, office server; released in 1.24.0). *Finish setting up* also lists the security settings a new server ships with off or unset, until each is done: two-step verification required for administrators and supervisors, and set up by each of them; scheduled backups with a copy off the server; the monthly restore drill (once backups run); the automatic sign-out after inactivity, saved and 15 minutes or less; a fingerprint or authenticator code to sign notes and approve time or spending; HTTPS; the audit log's safety copies off the database disk; and, on a server SUDS Server installed, the weekly host compliance check (its last result). Each says what it is and why, and its button opens Settings at that very setting, with its section open and the field selected. Nothing is ticked by hand: a step leaves the list as soon as the setting is saved, and comes back if someone turns it off again. Those marked as server settings (HTTPS, the audit anchors, the compliance check) are changed by whoever runs the server. **Settings › Security status** lists every step, done or not, under **Hardening checklist**, with two options worth knowing about (the authenticator allow-list and required single sign-on).

**Security findings** on a production server (administrators) are shown above the rest of Home as a sentence saying what is wrong and who fixes it, with a link to Security status — amber for a finding such as the audit log's safety copies (anchors) being on the same disk as the database, red when an audit check has actually failed or backups are off. They cannot be dismissed; they go when the configuration is fixed. **Load sample data** is offered on Home only on a server not in production mode (and on a device); a production server keeps it under Settings › Program › Sample data.

**Two-step verification.** If your role needs it and you have not set it up, a one-line bar says by when. Dismiss it (✕) and it becomes a small **🔐 2-step** link in the header, on every device, until you set it up. **Set up** (or My profile → **Set up 2-step verification**) shows a QR code for your authenticator app; enter its code and press **Turn on 2-step**. The bar goes as soon as it is on.

## Clients
* **Search** matches exact last name, "Last, First", phone number, date of birth (YYYY-MM-DD) or client code, plus the first few letters of a surname and (for names in the Latin alphabet) close misspellings. Either part of a double surname finds the person: *Vasquez* (or *Vas*) finds *Quintero-Vasquez*. When you choose a client in a form, the list opens only once you type, and closes when you pick someone or move on. Names in any script work — Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese and so on — and accents do not matter: *Oster* finds *Øster*, *Lecki* finds *Łecki*, *Jose* finds *José*. A name search lists the best matches first: the exact name, then the clients you have worked with lately, then names that start the same way, then look-alike spellings. The search box at the top of every page leaves the look-alikes out when anything matched better (a misspelling with no better match still finds them), also lists matching programs from the resource directory, and says what each result is (*Client* or *Resource*). Press **/** to jump to it (see *Keyboard shortcuts*).
* **Consent running out.** When one of a client's consents expires within 30 days, the top of their **Overview** says which one and on what date, with a link to the **Consents** tab to record a renewed one. A consent that has already expired while an open referral still relies on it is named there too.
* **New client** — opens **Quick add**: the first and last name (the only required fields), the name they go by, and a date of birth or phone if they give one (with the last name, those find an earlier record of the same person). **Create client** creates the record and opens it; **Add details** there carries on with the rest of the intake whenever you have it, in the same sections. **Full intake** in Quick add opens the whole intake form instead, with what you typed carried over (use it to set status *waitlist* for someone not yet enrolled). On the intake, **Who referred them to us** is how the person came to the program; the referrals you make to other providers are under **Referrals**.
* The client page shows six tabs, in the order a visit is worked, on every screen size: Overview, Visits, Notes, To-dos, Consents and Referrals — and, beside them, what your own work turns to most: **Care plan** and **Assessments** for clinicians, **Episodes** and **Care team** for supervisors (when the program uses those modules, and while there is room on the screen; at a very large text size some move under More). **More** holds the rest: Calls, Problems, Care plan, Assessments (clinicians and supervisors), SUPRT-A, Forms, Episodes, Requests, Time, Assistance $ (for roles that see the budget) and Care team. Problems, Care plan, Assessments and SUPRT-A appear when the program uses those modules and the client has something in them or you can add to them (see *Program profile and modules*). The **Overview** ends with **Recent activity** — the latest visits, calls, notes, referrals and to-dos in date order — and **All activity** there opens the whole history (it was the Timeline tab). See *Problem list, care plan and assessments* below.
* The buttons at the top of a client are **+ Log a visit**, **↻ Repeat last visit**, **+ Call**, **+ Text**, **+ Note**, **+ To-do** and **+ Make a referral** (the referral form opens for that client, with their consents). **On a phone** they fold into one **Add…** button that opens the same list.
* **Where things stand** (released in 1.22.0) is the first card on a client's **Overview**, on a phone too: the **last contact** (how long ago, the visit or call, the date and who), the **next to-do** (overdue in red, with how many more are open; it opens the *To-dos* tab) and the **open referrals**. It is read from the same activity as *Recent activity* at the foot of the page, so it says nothing that list does not: what you check before you see someone is at the top instead of some 3,000 px down on a phone.
* **Requests** records patient-rights requests (access, amendment, restriction, accounting of disclosures), each due 30 days after it was received. Mark one *Fulfilled* or *Denied* when answered; **Edit** corrects its kind, dates or notes (or reopens it); **Delete** is only for a request recorded in error — a request you are refusing is marked *Denied* so it stays on file. Edits and deletions are in the audit log.
* **Safety flags** (e.g. "no home visits alone") appear as a red badge on every page for that client.
* **Status and episodes.** Starting an episode of care makes the client *active* (including someone taken off the waitlist). While an episode is open, *Closed* and *Deceased* cannot be chosen on the Edit form: discharge on the Episodes tab instead — that closes the episode, ends the care team and clears open to-dos. The discharge dialog has no default reason; choose one. If you are not on the client's care team (you opened the episode yourself), closing it ends only your own part: the dialog says so before you submit, and afterwards *Episode closed — client stays open* names who is still on the care team; they or a supervisor discharge the client.
* **Who sees which clients.** From 1.16.0 navigators and clinicians see every client in the program, as supervisors and administrators do, and add their own visits, calls, notes, referrals and to-dos to any of them; changing or deleting what another worker recorded stays with supervisors and administrators. An administrator can hold a person to their own caseload (Settings → Users & permissions → **Permissions**, deny *See every client*), and from 1.17.0 the program can start every new navigator and clinician that way (see *Users & permissions* below); the Clients page then says *My clients* and the rest of this section applies to them.
* **After a discharge**, a caseload-scoped worker whose only link to the client was their assignment loses access to the record the next day (the discharge ends every assignment). If you need to look back at a discharged client — to answer a records request, or when they return — ask a supervisor to assign you again, or to look it up for you. Supervisors and administrators see every record.
* **Possible duplicates at intake.** While you type a new client's name, date of birth or phone, SUDS shows any matching record you can already open, and offers to re-admit a discharged earlier record of the same person when the surname and date of birth match (a phone number alone is never enough). The offer says only that an earlier record exists: not its client code, nor when or why the person was discharged, and a supervisor gets a task to review it. **Re-admit this person** asks why right there, under the offer (at least 8 characters, for example "walked in" — it is for the supervisor who reviews it), in the same dialog. The check is limited to 60 in 15 minutes per person. A match on someone else's caseload is never shown or counted to you (that would say the person is a client here): your intake goes ahead as usual and a supervisor gets a high-priority task on the existing record to compare the two and merge them if they are the same person. A new client entered on a phone or tablet is checked the same way when it syncs.
* **Duplicates.** Merge a duplicate from the Care team tab. The record merged away is kept, marked as merged, and an old link to it opens the record it was merged into. A record on **legal hold** cannot be merged, in either direction, until an administrator clears the hold.
* **Legal hold** can only be placed or cleared by an administrator (it usually comes from counsel). A supervisor who needs one placed should ask the administrator; the hold, its reason and who set it are in the audit log.
* **Contact details** are checked when saved: a date of birth cannot be in the future or before 1900, an email must look like one, and a phone number needs at least seven digits.

## Logging work
* **+ Log a visit** (on a client, on the Visits page, or **+ Log → Log a visit**) — the core service record, as a field note. The form shows what every visit has: who (optional for **outreach** and **naloxone distribution** — a kit handed to someone who gives no name counts in the funder report's community distribution; every other type needs the client), what you did (outreach, SBIRT screening, warm handoff, naloxone distribution, post-overdose follow-up…), when, the **supplies given** (naloxone kits and fentanyl test strips roll up into reports and update the client's naloxone status) and a short summary — seven fields at most on a phone, for any kind of visit. The rest is in sections you open when a visit needs them: **Where & how** (location and modality, filled in from your last visit; the folded heading says what they are, for example "Office · In person"), **Outcome & follow-up** (a follow-up date creates a to-do; see *Follow-up dates and your to-dos* below), **Syringe services**, **Funding & cost**, **Time** (duration and the time entry), **Recorded by** (supervisors) and **Add a note**. A section opens by itself when it holds something (an edit with a cost, say) or a field in it needs fixing, and otherwise stays as you last left it; a folded one says what it holds ("adds 30 min of direct service to your time").
* **An unsent visit is kept.** Everything typed into a new visit — every field, the supplies and a note written with it — is kept while the page stays open, if the dialog is closed by accident, you go to another page or you are signed out for inactivity. Opening **Log a visit** again asks **Resume your unsent visit?** (*Resume* puts it all back, *Discard* drops it), and after signing back in the same question is at the top of the page. It is kept in the page's memory only, never in the browser's storage, so reloading the page or closing the tab loses it; and it is yours only: if someone else signs in on that screen, it is gone, never shown to them.
* **The same visit twice?** Saving a new visit for a client who already has one of the same kind that day asks **Save another visit?** first, saying when it was recorded and by whom. **Save another** saves it (two visits in a day happen); **Cancel** goes back to the form with nothing saved. A new visit starts with your last visit's type, location and modality (the program's default location — **Street / Outdoor** for a harm-reduction program, **Office** for a treatment-adjacent one — until you have one).
* **Time from a visit.** *Also log this as a time entry* adds the visit's duration to your time (My time) as a draft to submit. It starts **unticked on every visit** (since 1.15.3; it used to start ticked, and a duration nobody checked went on time sheets): tick it when the visit should be on your time sheet, and it says how many minutes it adds. If the duration is still the 30 minutes the form starts with, Save asks **Log this time?** first — **Log 30 minutes**, or **Change the duration**, which takes you back to it. Supervisors approve hours from these entries, so tick it whenever the visit was paid time. A call works the same way (*Also log this as a time entry*, unticked). **Log time** by hand for a day (and client) that a visit or call already logged time for says so while you fill it in, and asks **Log this time as well?** before saving, so the same work is not counted twice (*Log it anyway* saves it: a second session with the same person is real time). The time list marks entries *(from visit)* and *(from call)*.
* **Possible duplicate time** (released in 1.24.0). When you save time that looks already logged — the same worker and day, with **overlapping times** (give a *Start time* to make this possible) or **the same minutes and the same description** — the time form asks *This may be time already logged*, shows the entry already there and why it matched, and offers three answers. **Merge** keeps the entry already there and adds this one's description to it, so the time counts once; if the times overlap you choose between **Combine both** (the kept entry then covers both ranges) and **Keep the existing entry's** times. **Save anyway** keeps both (it is recorded that you were warned). **Cancel** saves nothing and leaves the form open to change. Approved time cannot be merged into; ask a supervisor to reopen it first. Time saved on a device is never refused for this: it reaches the office marked **Possible duplicate**, shown on the time list and on the Supervision page's staff-time queue, where the worker, a manager or the approver presses **Merge** (keeps the earlier entry, combines the two) or **Not a duplicate**. The mark is the office's: on a phone or tablet that syncs with the office, **Not a duplicate** is pressed on the office SUDS (the device says so in its place); **Merge** works on the device too.
* **A note with the visit.** The short summary holds no names or health details. **Add a note** in the visit form writes the note there and then: the same kinds, formats and Part 2 flag as **+ Note**, saved with the visit in one step as a draft on the client's record, linked to the visit (sign it from Notes). It needs a client.
* **Supplies the program has no item for.** If Supplies keeps no naloxone kit or fentanyl test strip item, the visit form says so before you save — they count on the visit and in reports but are not taken off any stock — and offers a supervisor or administrator **Add naloxone kits and test strips** right there (everyone else is told to ask one). Kits are always entered in the one *Supplies given* list; the two plain number fields appear only when the program keeps no supply items at all.
* **Participant code on an anonymous contact** (1.17.0). An outreach or community-distribution contact with no client can carry a **Participant code**: the code the person builds the same way every time by your program's recipe (the form suggests one: the first two letters of their mother's first name, their birth month and the last two digits of their birth year, `MA0785`). Type it however they say it; it is kept in capitals, letters and digits only. It is offered only for a service that may have no client, and not with a client chosen (a client is already counted by their record). It is stored encrypted, shown only on the visit itself, never printed in a report or exported, and lets the **SSP report** count *Anonymous participants* — different people — without naming anyone ([docs/SUPPLIES.md](SUPPLIES.md), *Participant codes*).
* **+ Call** — inbound/outbound, who, duration, outcome (reached, voicemail…), crisis flag, encrypted summary, follow-up. *Also log this as a time entry* starts unticked, as on a visit. A **Remind me to call back on** date (*to follow up on*, for a text) puts a to-do on your list for that day: choosing a date ticks **Follow-up needed** for you (released in 1.22.0; before, a date with the box left unticked made no to-do, and SUDS now treats the date as the follow-up whichever way the call is saved). Unticking **Follow-up needed** on a call that had one turns the follow-up off and clears the date (released in 1.23.0). A call's or text's edit form has **Delete call** (**Delete text**) beside Cancel and Save, for whoever may change it (from 1.23.5): on a phone a tap on your own call opens that form, and Delete used to be only on the card a computer opens.
* **Follow-up dates and your to-dos** (released in 1.23.0). A follow-up date on a call or text, a visit (with a client) or a referral puts a to-do on the list of the worker who did the work, due that day, whether you set the date when you record it or add it later by **Edit**. Change the date and the to-do moves with it; clear the date (or untick **Follow-up needed** on a call) and the to-do is cancelled. Saving the record again never adds a second to-do. SUDS only changes a to-do it made while it is still as SUDS made it: open (not started), assigned to the record's worker, with its own title and the record's previous follow-up date. Once you have changed that to-do yourself (moved its date, renamed it, started it, handed it to someone else) it is yours: SUDS leaves it alone and makes no second one while it is open; and a to-do you have added details to is never cancelled — close it yourself. The same happens for work recorded on a phone or tablet, when it syncs. A referral always has a follow-up date (one is set from its urgency when you leave it empty); once the referral is closed or its outcome is recorded, changing the date makes no new to-do. Deleting the call, visit or referral cancels its follow-up to-do by the same rule (1.23.1): one still as SUDS made it goes with the record; one you have changed or added details to stays for you to close. From 1.23.3 the *Delete* question says so when the record has an open follow-up to-do (and, for a referral, a supervisor's reminder); from 1.23.4 it says the follow-up is cancelled when it is still as SUDS made it, and that it is left open only when it has been changed. From 1.23.2 this holds too when a phone changed the date and deleted the record before it synced, and deleting a referral also cancels a supervisor's open reminder to record its outcome. A call or text's to-do is titled with its **Purpose** ("Call back: Bed callback"); with none, **Call back** (or **Text back**) for the client, or who it was with in the form's words ("Call back: Family"), never a stored code (from 1.23.2).
* **Quick dates** (released in 1.22.0). A to-do's **Due** date has **Today**, **Tomorrow**, **In 3 days** and **In a week** under it; the follow-up dates on a visit, a call or text and a referral have **Tomorrow**, **In 3 days**, **In a week** and **In 2 weeks**. One tap sets the date (no time: due by the end of that day), instead of four or five in a phone's date picker. To push an overdue to-do back, open it and press **Tomorrow**.
* **Overdose & reversals** (sidebar) — record every overdose and naloxone reversal, including community ones with no client. *Where* starts where the program's visits do, and never offers Phone or Telehealth; *Funding source* starts on your default fund, as a visit's does; *Doses given* can be left empty, and the list then says *given, doses not recorded* (never a dose count nobody entered), the same 0 the funder report and the NDP log count. A message confirms the event was recorded, with the doses. **+ Log → Overdose or reversal** opens the same form from anywhere. Recording a **fatal** overdose for a client discharges them as deceased: after you confirm, their open episode is closed with the reason *deceased*, the care team ends and open to-dos are cancelled. If that was a mistake, change the outcome or **Delete** the event (in the event's dialog): the client goes back to the status they had, the episode is reopened, and the care team and to-dos are restored.
* **Time tracking** — non-client time (documentation, travel, meetings, training) is logged here; choose the funding source when the grant requires effort reporting.
* **To-dos** — follow-ups and reminders (**+ To-do** on a client, **+ Add a to-do** on the To-dos page); mark milestones (★) to show them in the client's recent activity (Overview) and **All activity**. A new to-do is always open: its form has no Status (released in 1.22.0); **Edit** on a to-do still has it. **Assigned to me** and **Overdue** above the list are on/off buttons: one that is on shows a check mark (✓), and a screen reader says it is pressed (from 1.23.5; the same for the filters on Calls & texts and the choices on Spending, Overdose response and Reports). Ticking a supervisor's *Finish and sign* reminder done while you still have draft notes on its client's record asks first ("You still have N unsigned draft notes on this record. Mark the reminder done anyway?"); **Cancel** leaves it open (from 1.23.5, on Home and the To-dos list).
  A to-do that came from a call, a visit or a referral (a follow-up, or a supervisor's reminder) opens it: **Open the call**, **Open the visit** or **Open the referral** is on its row and in **Edit to-do** (on a phone it is on the row too, from 1.23.2; tap the rest of the row for the to-do). A supervisor's reminder to record a referral's outcome opens the referral's **Record outcome** form (released in 1.23.1).

## Street outreach
**Street outreach** (**+ Log → Street outreach contact**, or the sidebar) is one screen for logging field contacts on a phone, one-handed: no client, no names.
* **Contact**: the kind of contact — *Outreach* or *Naloxone distribution*, as your program words them under Settings → Lists. These are the services that need no client.
* **Supplies given**: a − count + row for naloxone kits, fentanyl (and xylazine) test strips, syringes, wound care and the program's other usual items, big enough for a thumb; **Another item** adds any other item from Supplies. The supplies come off the stock of the site they came from, the batch that expires soonest first, exactly as a visit's do. If Supplies keeps no naloxone or test strip item, the kits and strips are counted on the contact but not taken off any stock (the row says so).
* **Participant code (optional)**: only if the person gives one, the SSP participant code they build the same way every time (as on the visit form; never a name). It is stored encrypted, and **My shift** says how many different codes you saw, never the codes.
* **Participant code first** (released in 1.21.0). When your administrator turns on **Settings › Program › Minimal personal information › Outreach records use a participant code by default**, Street outreach asks for the code right under the kind of contact; **New client** asks for the participant code and keeps the name, date of birth and phone behind **Add a name** (the person's name can be added later on their record); and a new visit with no client starts with its participant code open. A client known only by a code is shown as *Participant* and the code, found by typing the code in the client search, and counted like any other client (the syringe services summary counts the same code on an anonymous contact once).
* **Field device** (released in 1.21.0). If your phone is a field device, This device says **Field device: holds only …**: your own clients assigned or seen recently (name, participant code and safety flags), your contacts, your to-dos, supplies and lists. Notes, documents, consents and intake details stay at the office. Your administrator decides this under Settings › Synced devices; you can choose it yourself when you first sync (**Keep only what I need in the field**), but only an administrator can change it back.
* **Where**: roughly where (*Street / Outdoor*, *Shelter*, *Community*…), never an address; and, when the program has more than one supply site, which one the supplies came from (the van, say). Both stay set for the next contact.
* **Notes (optional)**: a line about what happened, **without names, nicknames, descriptions or anything else that could identify someone**. They are encrypted like every visit summary and never shown in the shift summary.
* **Save contact** saves it as an anonymous visit: it is in *Visits*, the funder report's community distribution, the NDP log (*Community member (anonymous)*) and the syringe services summary, like any anonymous visit. The counts go back to 0 for the next contact; the kind of contact and the place stay. **Save contact** stays at the foot of the screen while you fill in the counts, within reach of a thumb (released in 1.22.0).
* **Undo** (released in 1.22.0). For 10 seconds after a contact is saved, the message *Contact saved: …* has **Undo**: it takes the contact back (saved twice, or with the wrong count) — it is no longer counted, in My shift or anywhere else, and its supplies go back on the stock. It is the same as deleting your own visit under *Visits*, and is in the audit log the same way. The cursor stays on the form for the next contact. Undo also puts **Same as last contact** back to what you handed out on the contact before, or hides it if there was none (released in 1.23.0).
* **Same as last contact** (released in 1.22.0). After your first contact, a button above the counts says what you handed out last time (*↻ Same as last contact: 1 Naloxone kit, 2 Fentanyl test strips*); one tap fills those counts in, and you change any before saving. It remembers only the items and how many — nothing about the person — for your account, and a contact with nothing handed out leaves it as it was.
* **My shift** under the form counts what you have logged since the start of today, or since you pressed **Start a new shift**: contacts, naloxone kits, test strips, the supplies handed out, and your latest contacts. Only your own contacts, and never their notes.
* **With no connection.** On SUDS on this device, and on a phone that keeps an offline copy of the office's SUDS, the screen works with no signal at all: the contact is saved on the device, the device's stock goes down, and (for an office's copy) it goes to the office at the next sync, where the office takes the supplies off its own stock once. On a phone that syncs with the office, the top of every page says whether anything is still waiting to go (released in 1.22.0): **⇅ 3 to send** (on a phone, **⇅ 3**) while changes are on the device only, **Synced 2d ago** (**⇅ ✓**) once everything has gone, **Not synced yet** before the first sync. It opens **This device**, where **Sync now** is.
* **No signal on the office app** (released in 1.23.0). If your phone uses the office SUDS (not an offline copy) and loses signal, **Save contact** keeps a contact that names nobody — no participant code and no notes — on the phone: *No signal: the contact … is kept on this phone*. The top of every page says **N contacts waiting to send** (on a phone, **⏳ N**), and Street outreach lists them under **Waiting to send**. They are sent by themselves when you are back online, or the next time you sign in, and each is counted once, its supplies taken off the stock once, however many times it is sent. **Send now** tries again at once (if your session has ended meanwhile, it asks you to sign in, and they are sent once you have); **Discard** removes one entered by mistake (it is never sent). A contact with a participant code or notes is **not** kept on the phone — SUDS keeps nothing that could identify a person in the browser of a phone others may use — so it stays in the form: save it again when you have signal, or press **Keep it without the code and notes**. The waiting contacts are your own: if someone else signs in on the phone they neither see nor send them. Undo is not offered for a waiting contact; Discard it instead. If the office had in fact received a waiting contact before the signal went, and it was deleted there since, sending it does not record it again: SUDS says *1 waiting contact was not recorded: it had been undone since it was first saved* (1.23.1).
* **Set up this phone for the field** (released in 1.23.0). The offline banner and the foot of Street outreach link to it. It says what a **field device** keeps (your own recent clients' names, participant codes and safety flags; your contacts, to-dos, supplies and lists; never notes, documents, consents or intake details) and who decides: where your office allows offline copies, making a phone a field device narrows what it holds, so you may do it yourself as you set the phone up (**Open the offline copy of SUDS**, then **Keep only what I need in the field** on This device › Sync); only an administrator can widen it again. Where it does not, the page says only that an administrator decides first (from 1.23.2). **Ask my administrator** gives every administrator a to-do; the page then says *Asked*, *Approved* or *Not approved*. Where your office has not allowed offline copies, asking is the only way. Administrators answer under **Settings › Synced devices › Field-device requests**: **Approve** makes every device the person syncs from a field device; **Decline** changes nothing. Either way every administrator's to-do for the request is closed, with a line saying who answered and how; if the person's account is deactivated while the request is open, the request and those to-dos are closed with a line saying so (1.23.1). If offline copies are still off on the office server when the request is approved (from 1.23.1), the administrator is told so before and after approving, with the setting that turns them on (`LOCAL_MODE_ENABLED=true`, or `"localModeEnabled": true` in `data/server.json`, then restart SUDS), and your page says *Approved, but not ready yet*: your account is set as a field account, but your office has not turned on offline copies, so ask your SUDS administrator to turn them on.
* **Start page.** Tick **Open SUDS on this screen when I sign in** under the form (or choose it under **My profile → Start page**) and SUDS opens on Street outreach after you sign in, on every device you use.

## Problem list, care plan and assessments (CalAIM)
These are the **care plan & problem list** and **assessments** modules: they are shown when the program is treatment-adjacent or an administrator has switched them on (see *Program profile and modules*). They follow the CalAIM Behavioral Health Documentation Redesign: a current **problem list**, **progress notes** that say which problems they address, a **care coordination plan**, and assessment by the six **ASAM dimensions** (SUDS's six-dimension assessment is ASAM-aligned; it does not include the ASAM Criteria). How each requirement maps to SUDS, and what is out of scope, is in `docs/compliance/CALAIM.md`.

* **Overview → Clinical picture** sums it up: active problems, how many care plan goals are active and any review that is overdue (in red), the latest six-dimension assessment (the six ratings and the level of care), and each outcome measure's latest score with its trend. A PHQ-9 whose question 9 was answered above "Not at all" shows a red safety banner at the top. Each part appears only for roles that may see it.
* **Problems** — the client's current problems and needs, in plain words. **+ Problem** adds one, optionally with an **ICD-10-CM code** (typed in; SUDS checks the format — a letter, two characters, then optionally a dot and up to four more — and tidies it, so *f1120* becomes *F11.20*; there is no code look-up) and **social determinant (Z) codes** (open *Social determinants* to tick common Z55–Z65 codes such as Z59.02 Unsheltered homelessness, or type others). Set where it came from (self-report, assessment, referral) and when it started. Problems are never deleted: **Resolve** one (it is dated today) or make it *inactive*; **History** shows every change — who, when, and the old and new values. Everyone on the care team can keep the list; an administrator can read it.
* **Notes → Problems this note addresses.** The note form lists the client's active problems; tick the ones the service was about. The note shows them under *Addresses*, and the Problems tab counts the notes for each.
* **Care plan** — **+ Goal** records a goal *in the client's own words*, tied to a problem, with a target date and a **review date**. Once the review date passes, the goal (and the Overview) says *Review overdue*; **Reviewed** records that you went over it with the client today and sets the next review. **+ Step** adds what will be done, by whom (client, staff, family/support, another provider) and by when; tick *Also add it to the to-do list* to put a staff step on someone's to-do list, and marking the step **Done** closes that to-do. Mark a goal *Met*, *Partially met*, *Not met* or *Discontinued* rather than deleting it (delete is only for a goal entered in error, by whoever added it or a supervisor). **Print** produces a one-page plan with the active problems, goals and steps, and lines for signatures.
* **Assessments** (clinicians and supervisors) — **+ Six-dimension assessment** (ASAM-aligned) records a 0–4 risk rating for each of the six dimensions with what supports it, the **recommended** level of care, the level **referred to**, and — required when they differ — why (waitlist, client preference, not available…). SUDS records the ratings and the decision; use your program's own licensed ASAM Criteria materials for the rating definitions. SUDS does not include the ASAM Criteria; "ASAM" is a trademark of the American Society of Addiction Medicine, and a program that uses the Criteria needs its own licence from ASAM. The latest assessment sets the client's *ASAM level* field and is shown on the Overview; earlier ones stay in the history.
* **Outcome measures** — **+ PHQ-9**, **+ GAD-7**, **+ AUDIT-C** and **+ Wellbeing (0–10)** open the questionnaire. **+ DAST-10** appears only once an administrator has turned it on under **Settings → Screening instruments**, confirming that the program holds the rights to use it (its licence covers non-commercial clinical use with credit); results already recorded stay visible if it is turned off again. The score and its band appear as soon as every question is answered and are saved with the answers (encrypted). For AUDIT-C, choose the cut-off (men 4+, women 3+; unspecified uses 3+). Give the same measure again over time: the tab and the Overview show the trend. Click a result to see the answers; **Delete** is for a questionnaire entered in error.
* **PHQ-9 question 9.** Any answer above "Not at all" raises a **safety alert** when the PHQ-9 is saved, whatever the total: an urgent to-do for today is put on your list, and the alert offers the client's signed safety plan (or **Write a safety plan now**, which opens a note in the *Safety plan* format). Follow your program's crisis protocol; for imminent danger call 988 or 911.
* **Program outcomes** are on **Reports → Outcome measures**: for each measure, how many clients were screened, how many were screened twice or more in the period, the mean first and last scores, and the number and percentage who improved (lower is better for PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C and DAST-10; higher for wellbeing). **Export (de-identified CSV)** gives one row per client and measure, keyed by a record id drawn at random for that file (not the client code), with the year rather than dates and no answers or names.
* Instruments not included: BAM, BARC-10 and other questionnaires whose licence for use in software could not be confirmed. A program that holds a licence can set one up as a county form (**Forms**).
* SUDS does **not** do billing, claims, e-prescribing (eRx) or medication administration records (eMAR).

## Referrals and resources
The **Resource Directory** holds treatment providers, MAT clinics, shelters, harm reduction, legal aid, transport, etc. Keep *Last verified* current (entries older than 6 months are flagged). The **Referrals we make** page (and the client's Referrals tab) holds the referrals the program makes to other providers — who referred a client *to* the program is on their intake. Create a **referral** (**+ Referral**), link the **consent** that authorizes sharing — it must name that provider (or its organization) — then update the status as it moves: pending → contacted → accepted/waitlisted → scheduled → admitted → completed. The outcome and any barrier are recorded when it closes (**Record outcome**), so a new referral asks for them only when it is entered as already over.

* **No consent names the provider?** Under *Consent*, **Record a consent naming …** turns the referral dialog to the consent form filled in for that provider — a Part 2 consent to disclose, the provider as recipient, *Referral and care coordination* as the purpose, and what the program's usual consent covers and when it expires. Check it against the signed form and save: you are back in the referral, as you left it, with the new consent chosen. **Back to the referral** (or Escape) returns without recording one.
* **Changing only the follow-up date or the notes** of a referral the provider has already been told about never asks for a consent: nothing new goes to the provider. Changing its status, provider, appointment, urgency or what is shared still needs one (or another lawful basis) when none has been recorded for it yet (released in 1.23.1).
* **Provider not listed?** *＋ Add a provider that is not on this list…* asks for its name and category (and a phone and city if you have them) in the same dialog, adds it to the directory and chooses it; finish its profile under Resources later.
* The **Referrals** list shows the client's name to anyone who can open the client (the code for everyone else). Its *Consent* column says **Consent on file** only when a live Part 2 consent names that provider; otherwise **No Part 2 consent** (a general release, or a consent naming someone else, does not count).

**Secure referral links (office server, 1.17.0).** Off until an administrator switches them on (Privacy & Part 2 → *Secure referral links*), after counsel has reviewed them; the provider's browser must be able to reach the office server over the internet. To send a referral to a provider that does not use SUDS, open the referral → **Secure link**. With a live Part 2 consent naming that provider you can send the **referral** itself: the client's name, the reason and urgency (write only what the provider needs), and their phone and date of birth only if you tick them. Without one you can send only a **contact notice**, which names nobody and asks the provider to call the program quoting a reference. Choose how long it lasts (1, 3 or 7 days). The link and, for a referral, a six-digit **access code** are shown once: send the link by email or text and **give the code another way** (by phone). The first browser that enters the code claims the link; five wrong codes lock it; you or a supervisor can **withdraw** it at any time. The consent is checked again every time the link is opened (and a link is not opened once the referral is closed or the record removed), and the disclosure goes into the client's accounting when the provider first opens it. When the provider answers (received, accepted, scheduled, declined, could not reach) you get a to-do on the referral: confirm it and record the outcome yourself — SUDS does not change the referral's status. Not available on SUDS on this device or a device's offline copy. Details: `docs/security/REFERRAL-LINKS.md`.

**Provider pictures.** After adding a starter directory, **Download provider pictures** replaces each program's generated card with the picture from the provider's own website: on an office server it downloads them (the server needs internet access), on SUDS on this device it uses the copies the site was published with. When it finishes it says how many pictures it got — on screen and in a pop-up message — and, under "Why some had no picture", why the rest keep their generated card. On a phone or tablet that syncs with an office server the button is not shown: the office's pictures arrive with the next sync. You can always add your own photos on a program's page (**+ Add pictures**).

**Pictures on a program's page.** A program can have up to 12 pictures; the first is its main picture and the directory card's cover. There are four ways to add one, and all of them save the picture the same way (shrunk on your device first):
* **+ Add pictures** opens your computer's or phone's file window. That window belongs to the operating system and can open behind the browser; the card says when one has opened.
* **Drag** picture files from a folder or another window onto the Pictures card (it is outlined while you drag), or **paste** a copied picture (Ctrl+V / ⌘V) anywhere on the program's page outside a text box.
* **Add from a web address** takes the address of a picture (https:// only), with an optional caption. On an office server the server downloads it — or, given the address of a web page, the preview picture that page advertises — with the same safety checks as provider pictures: never an address on the office network, JPEG, PNG or WebP only, 2 MB at most. The audit log records the site's name, not the full address. On SUDS on this device, and on a phone or tablet copy, your browser fetches the picture itself, and only from the site SUDS is served from (its Content-Security-Policy allows nothing else, and most websites would refuse anyway): otherwise you see *Pictures from other sites cannot be copied from this browser* — save the picture to the device and use **+ Add pictures**, or drag it onto the card. (On a device, a plain http:// address is accepted only for the site SUDS itself is served from, or for this computer.)

Do not add pictures of clients.

## Incoming referrals
*Released in 1.24.0.* **Incoming referrals** (in the menu; under *More* on a phone) is the intake queue: people referred **to** your program by an emergency department or hospital, a jail's re-entry team, a detox or withdrawal-management unit, probation, parole or a court, another provider, the person themselves or their family. They are not clients yet. It is for navigators, clinicians, supervisors and administrators (*See incoming referrals* and *Work incoming referrals* in Settings → Users & permissions); finance and read-only never see it.

* **Record one** with **+ Incoming referral** on the queue, or **+ Log → Incoming referral** anywhere: who referred them (and the organization), how it arrived (phone, fax, email, walk-in, eReferral), when, how urgent (routine, soon, urgent), the reason and needs, the person's name, date of birth and phone (a name or a phone is enough), and, folded away, the referrer's own name, phone and email. Assign it to someone now or later. Everything about the person and the referrer is encrypted; recording it shares nothing with anyone.
* **Home** shows *N new referrals* (red when one is urgent) and *N incoming referrals assigned to you*; each opens the queue. The queue filters by status, by who it is assigned to (anyone, me, nobody yet, a colleague) and urgency, and searches by the exact surname or the referring organization. Open referrals list the urgent first, then the longest waiting.
* **Everyone who works intake sees every open referral**, including a worker held to their caseload: a referred person is nobody's client yet. To keep someone out of it, deny them *See incoming referrals*. Once a referral is accepted it belongs to the client's record: a worker held to their caseload sees it only if that client is on their caseload.
* **Log an attempt** for each call, text, email, visit or letter, and what happened (reached them, left a message, no answer, wrong number). The first attempt moves a *New* referral to *Contacting* and sets its **time to first contact** (from when it was received). The queue's header and the Supervision page show the median over the last 90 days and how many were tried within 24 hours.
* **Accept** when they become a client. SUDS first shows existing records that match their name and date of birth, full name or phone — only records you can open, as at intake — with **Link to …**; or find the client with the search box; or **Create a new client from this referral**, which opens *New client — full intake* filled in from the referral (with *Who referred them to us* and the referral date), and links it when you save. The duplicate check runs there as usual. An accepted referral shows which client it became, and stays closed.
* **Close without a client** as *Declined* (say why), *Unable to reach*, or *Referred elsewhere* (say where). **Reopen** puts a closed one back on the queue (not an accepted one).
* **SUDS never tells the referrer anything.** Receiving a referral is not a disclosure. Telling a hospital, a court or probation that the person is now a client here would be one: do it only under the person's consent, recorded on their Consents tab, as for any other disclosure.
* The queue is kept at the office. On a phone or tablet that syncs with the office it is not available (open the office SUDS); SUDS on this device keeps its own. A referral closed without a client is deleted after the program's retention period from when it closed; an accepted one is kept, and deleted, with the client's record.

## Supervision
A clinician does not countersign by role. A clinical supervisor who is a clinician (an LCSW who supervises associates, for example) needs **Countersign notes** (`notes:cosign`) granted to them individually: Settings → Users & permissions → their **Permissions**, which says so for every clinician who does not hold it (1.25.2). They then countersign and have the Supervision page. Only a note's author signs it: a supervisor opening someone else's draft is not offered **Sign & lock**, and countersigns once the author has signed (1.25.2).

The **Supervision** page is the work waiting on a supervisor: notes to countersign, unsigned drafts across the team, staff time to approve, open client rights requests, and referrals with no outcome recorded. With a passkey on your device, approving time (and spending, on Budget) offers **Confirm with fingerprint and approve**, which records that you approved it with your fingerprint; you can still approve without it unless your programme requires a fingerprint or authenticator code. Approved time is part of a signed-off time sheet and cannot be edited or deleted, by the worker or a manager (on a device too); to correct it, a supervisor opens **My time**, presses **Reopen** on the entry and gives a reason, and the worker corrects it and submits it again.
* **Unsigned notes across your team** — each draft has **Open note** (the note opens over the page, as for countersigning) and **Remind author**, which gives its author a to-do on that client's record, due today (high priority when the draft is overdue), asking them to finish and sign it. The to-do is titled *Finish and sign your draft notes* (from 1.23.4, with its client shown beside it as for every to-do; *…for <client>* in 1.23.3, and it named one note before), nothing from the notes themselves (and, from 1.23.2, no record id: its last line says it closes once their draft notes on that client's record are signed); like every to-do its title and details are encrypted, and it is in the audit log. Once a reminder is open the row shows **Sent** with its date instead of the button, whoever sent it; one reminder covers that author's drafts on that client's record, and it closes by itself once they are all signed (from 1.23.2), or is cancelled if the last of them is deleted instead (from 1.23.3); when the author closes the to-do, a new one can be sent. You are never offered a reminder for your own draft, nor (from 1.23.5) for the draft of someone whose account is no longer active: the row says *Author no longer active*, and **Remind all overdue authors** leaves them out. Moving a caseload (Supervision tools → Move a caseload; Settings for an administrator) cancels the departing worker's reminders rather than handing them on (1.23.4), and from 1.23.5 its result says how many it cancelled; their drafts stay theirs. The author's reminder has **Open <client>'s notes**, which opens the client's Notes tab on their own drafts there (**Show all notes** goes back); tapping it on Home goes straight there (from 1.23.3). Signing or deleting a draft there stays on their drafts, and once none is left it says *No draft notes left to sign on this record.* (from 1.23.4). Deleting their last draft on that record says the reminder is cancelled too. From 1.24.0 the reminder also names the draft it was sent about and offers **Open the draft** first, which opens that note over the page you are on, ready to edit and **Sign & lock**: on the to-do, on its row on a phone, and when you tap its title on Home. **Remind author** links the reminder to that row's draft; **Remind all overdue authors** links each reminder to that author's oldest overdue draft on the client. It still covers all their drafts there and closes the same way (once the last is signed, or cancelled if the last is deleted): if the linked draft is signed or deleted while others are left, the reminder stays open and offers **Open <client>'s notes** for the rest. Only a supervisor who may countersign notes can link a reminder to a draft, and only to the author's own draft on that client; opening it is checked as for any note. Only whoever sent a reminder (or someone else who may send one) can give it to another worker or move it to another client (from 1.23.4): the author can change its date, priority or details, not who or which client it is about; from 1.23.5 the author's form shows **Assigned to** and **Client** greyed out, with that reason under them, rather than refusing them on Save. Only someone who may countersign notes can send such a reminder (from 1.23.3): a to-do anyone else writes with its last line is refused, and one written before 1.23.3 by anyone else is an ordinary to-do, not shown as **Sent** here.
* **Referrals needing attention › Waiting to hear what happened** lists the referrals nobody has recorded an outcome for: **No answer yet** (the provider was told about the client and has not replied) and **Appointment set** (the client had an appointment, with its date, and nobody has said whether it happened). A referral the provider **accepted** or put on its **waiting list** is not listed: that is the provider's answer, and the referral stays open on the worker's own list, with its follow-up to-do, until it closes. The oldest is first, with how long it has waited ("12 days") and **Made by** — the worker who made it, the person to ask what happened (released in 1.22.0). Each row has the same kind of buttons as an unsigned note (released in 1.23.0):
  * **Open referral** opens the client's Referrals tab (shown when you may open the client).
  * **Remind worker** gives the worker who made it a to-do on that client's record, due today: *Record what happened with your referral to* the provider, saying who asked and when the referral was sent, nothing from the referral's notes or outcome; like every to-do its title and details are encrypted, and it is in the audit log. It is linked to the referral, so recording the outcome closes it, with the referral's follow-up to-do — whether by **Record outcome** or by editing the referral to a status that is its outcome (admitted, completed, declined, closed). The worker's to-do has **Open the referral**, which opens the referral's **Record outcome** form (released in 1.23.1). Once a reminder is open the row shows **Sent** with its date instead of the button, whoever sent it. Only a supervisor of that worker's team is offered it (everyone, for a supervisor who may see all clients; otherwise only staff who name you as their supervisor), never for your own referral or a closed account.
  * **Record outcome** opens the referral's outcome form, for a role that may record outcomes (pressing the row does the same; without that permission the row opens the referral).
* **Remind all overdue authors** sends one reminder for each author and client with an overdue draft after you confirm (one per overdue draft until 1.23.2); the confirmation says how many it will send and how many notes are skipped because a reminder for them is still open. Pressing it again sends nothing new.

* **Clinical notes** — clinicians and supervisors write and read them; navigators can read them but not write, sign or add to them (from 1.16.0, unless an administrator has turned that off for you); use SOAP/DAP/BIRP/GIRP formats (sections auto-build the narrative).

With no usual format yet, a clinical note starts as a **Progress** note (from 1.23.2; a clinician's new note is a clinical one) and an administrative note as a narrative. ## Consents (42 CFR Part 2)
Your program is a **42 CFR Part 2** program: the client header says so, and the rules below are enforced — SUDS refuses what the rule does not allow and tells you why.

* **Give the patient notice.** When a client starts, give them the program's notice of privacy practices (Privacy & Part 2 → Patient notice has the current version to print) and record **+ Notice given** on their Consents tab. Until you do, the Overview says *Not recorded* and Home counts them.
* **Record the consent.** **+ Consent** starts on the single consent for **treatment, payment and health care operations** (for a referral, use **Record a consent naming …** in the referral form instead), with the wording the rule allows. Tick what information it covers — the ticks are what SUDS enforces (an automated disclosure shares only those), and they are written into the consent as its scope; anything else the signed form says about what it covers goes in the optional note below them (kept, not enforced) — then when it expires, who signed (the client, or a parent, guardian or representative — then their name), and tick each statement only if the signed form really contains it: the right to revoke in writing, the redisclosure statement, and what happens if the client refuses to sign. Use a separate consent for **SUD counseling notes** or for use in a **legal proceeding** — each of those covers nothing else. A general **ROI** is not a Part 2 consent. **Print** on a consent gives a PDF of everything recorded. A consent marked *Pre-2024 form* is still in force; renew it on the current form when you next see the client.
* **A consent covers only who it names.** A referral, a disclosure or a file under a consent is refused unless the consent names the recipient (case, spacing and punctuation do not matter; a referral is checked against the provider's name and its organisation). On the treatment, payment and operations consent, write the providers' names, e.g. *County Behavioral Health, Riverbend OTP and my other treating providers*: the class wording on its own names nobody SUDS can check. If a consent really does cover a recipient SUDS cannot match, a supervisor can override, with a written reason that is kept with the disclosure.
* **Record every disclosure.** Referrals record their own. For anything else shared, **+ Disclosure**: who, what, why, how and on what basis. When you share under a consent, SUDS shows the **§2.32 notice** — put it on the fax cover, letter or email. Tick *includes SUD counseling notes* or *for use in a legal proceeding* when that is what it is; SUDS then asks for the consent or court order that covers it.
* **Without consent.** A referral can rest only on the client's consent, a medical emergency (with a written reason), a court order, or a supervisor's justified override. On **+ Disclosure**: a disclosure to a qualified service organization, or for research or an audit, needs the agreement or approval with that organization on file (Privacy & Part 2 → Agreements); research, audits, reports of a crime on the premises and child-abuse reports are recorded by a supervisor, with a written reason for the last two.
* A consent marked *Cannot authorise a disclosure* is missing a required element (for example one that came from a device without them): record a new one.
* **Courts and subpoenas.** A subpoena alone never allows you to share a client's record. A supervisor records the **court order** on the Consents tab (Court orders); only an order with the required findings and notice can be relied on. Ask your supervisor and county counsel before responding to any legal request.
* **Agreed restrictions.** If a client's restriction request has been agreed (Requests tab), SUDS asks you to confirm you have checked it before anything is shared.
* **Revoke** a consent when the client withdraws it: open referrals that relied on it are flagged.

## Privacy & Part 2 (supervisors and administrators)
The **Privacy & Part 2** page (sidebar) has the program-level controls; navigators and clinicians see the patient notice and the list of their clients who have not been given it.
* **Overview** — counts of what needs attention; an administrator sets whether this is a Part 2 program and the size of identified export that opens a draft incident. Switching Part 2 off needs a written reason; it is audited, shown to administrators on Home until switched back on, and opens a draft incident for the privacy officer.

* **Patient notice** — the notice of privacy practices, ready to print. An administrator edits it (each save is a new version) once counsel has approved the wording.

* **Incidents & breaches** — record anything that may have exposed client information the day it is discovered. Fill in the four-factor risk assessment, make the determination (a breach is presumed unless the assessment shows a low probability of compromise), link the affected clients and record each notice as it goes out (a client whose record is later purged stays listed by code, as breach documentation). The deadline (60 days from discovery) and whether HHS and the media must be told are worked out for you; Home warns two weeks before and when late. ## Changing the choices on forms (administrators)
**Settings → Lists** holds the drop-down choices on documentation forms, grouped by form and named the way the forms name them: *What did you do?*, Location, Modality and Outcome on visits; *Who* and the call and text outcomes; referral *Status / What happened* and *If it did not happen, why*; the overdose form's *What happened* and *Given by*; time categories; note formats; primary substance; reasons for discharge. An administrator (the `settings:manage` permission) can, for each list:

* **Reword a choice.** Type the new wording and press Save (or Enter). Forms, lists, filters, reports and Excel/CSV exports all use it. Only the wording changes: records keep the same stored value, so old records and reports mean what they always did. Clear the box and save to go back to the built-in wording.
* **Reorder** with the ↑ and ↓ buttons.
* **Hide (retire) a choice** the program does not use. It is no longer offered on new records; records that already have it still show it, and editing one keeps it (shown as *no longer offered*). **Show** brings it back.
* **Add a choice** of your own. SUDS makes its stored value from the wording, and never one that a built-in choice already uses. A choice you added can be reworded or hidden like any other.
* **Restore defaults**: the built-in wording and order, with every built-in choice offered again. Choices you added are hidden, not deleted, because records may use them.

Choices marked **Used by SUDS** drive a count or an automatic step — for example a call *reached* or a text *replied* counts as contact with the client, a *fatal* overdose or a *deceased* discharge marks the client deceased, outreach and naloxone distribution can be recorded without a client — so they can be reworded but not hidden. Referral statuses and the overdose *What happened* choices can be reworded and reordered but not added to, because each one means something to the consent check and the counts. Race and ethnicity, ASAM level, stage of change, consent types, patient-rights requests and funding types are national or legal code sets and are not editable; the page lists them and says why.

An administrator sees a small **Edit this list** link beside each of these drop-downs on the forms themselves, which opens that list. Staff see changes the next time they sign in or reload. The lists belong to the office SUDS: a device that syncs with it receives them and cannot change them; SUDS on this device (with no office) keeps its own.

**Funding sources** are at the bottom of the same tab for anyone who manages the budget (`budget:manage`: administrators, supervisors, finance — finance uses **Funding & spending**, which has the same controls): add one quickly (name, type, period and total award; budget lines and the rest are on Funding & spending), rename it, or deactivate it so it is no longer offered on new records (records already charged to it keep it). A **Manage** link beside every *Funding source* drop-down opens it.

## Reports and Excel
Choose a date range for program summaries and monthly trends. The dates are calendar days where the program is (**Settings → Program → Your program → Organisation time zone**, else the server's `ORG_TIMEZONE`; SUDS on this device starts with the browser's time zone), so an evening visit on the last day of a quarter is in that quarter. ## Prevention events
**Prevention** (sidebar; under **More** for navigators and clinicians) is for SABG primary prevention: presentations, workshops, trainings, community events, media campaigns, coalition meetings, drug-free alternatives, screening events and policy work — work with groups, not services to a person. **+ Record an event** asks the date, what it was, the **kind of event**, its **strategy (CSAP)** — information dissemination, education, alternatives, problem identification and referral, community-based process or environmental — and its **population (IOM category)** — universal direct, universal indirect (media, for example), selective or indicated — then the audience, where, the **attendance** (how many people came or were reached: a count, never names; tick *estimate* for a campaign's reach), the hours, the fund and notes (stored encrypted). Anyone who records visits records events; an event is its worker's to change or delete, or a supervisor's or administrator's, and anyone else opens it read-only. The kinds of event and audiences are lists your administrator can change (**Settings → Lists → Prevention**); the CSAP strategies and IOM categories can be reworded but not added to, and *Training* cannot be retired.

**Activity summary** (the Prevention page's second tab, and **Reports → Prevention activity summary**; anyone who can open Reports): for a period, the events, hours, attendance and **people trained** (the attendance of training events), by strategy, by IOM category, by the two together and by kind of event, as **Excel** or **CSV** (with *Export*). Attendance is added up across events, so someone at two events counts twice. It is **not a PPSDS file**: SUDS has not mapped its fields to DHCS's Primary Prevention SUD Data Service, which awaits the DHCS PPSDS data dictionary — key PPSDS from the summary, and check it against your current SABG prevention reporting requirements.

## Funder report, funding attribution and harm-reduction reports
**Funder report.** Counts people, each once. The first thing on the page says what kind of report it is. A supervisor, an administrator or **finance** (the person who usually writes the grant report; finance sees the counts, never a client record) opens it as **Submission to your funder — not for publication**, for any range or one funding source: exact counts, every figure the true number, which is what a funder asks for at quarter end (choose **Counts → Small cells suppressed** if you would rather hide small groups; the file says which). Publishing or sharing figures beyond the funder is a separate step, **Prepare a publication release** (below). Two plain lines under the heading say how the figures were counted; **Why some numbers are hidden** (or **About these counts**) opens the full statement, which is also on the file's About sheet. In a suppressed report or a publication release, every count of people with fewer than the small-cell threshold (11; **Settings → Program → Reporting**) shows as `<11` so nobody in a small group can be picked out — breakdown rows (race, gender, language…), people per fund, overdose events and reversals by month (a publication release gives the period's overdose events as totals and the reversals by month; the events by month are in the submission only), discharge reasons, and a total that is itself small — and wherever a hidden figure could still be worked out from the other figures in the report, more is hidden and shows as `suppressed` (the total too, only when nothing else will do; a breakdown too small to show safely is withheld). Kits, test strips, services, hours and money are exact; naloxone doses are exact unless they would show how many reversals a hidden cell holds. In a publication release, funds that served fewer people than the threshold are listed together in one row, **Other funds (*n* combined)**, with how many funds it holds and their staff hours; its people and services show as `withheld`. The NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page count the same way. In a publication release every month of the period and every "given by" and discharge reason is listed, even when it is 0, rare typed-in values (a language only a few people speak) beyond the first ten are combined as *Other (combined)*, and a table whose protection SUDS cannot confirm is **withheld** and listed with the reason, while the rest is published: publish each period once, never a period that overlaps or contains another you have published, and look over what is withheld before you share it. People served is never hidden while new admissions or episodes opened are shown: they are hidden with it. Small cells are screened automatically, which is not a guarantee: before a publication release's file downloads you tick **I have reviewed the withheld and small figures before sharing** (recorded in the audit log). SUDS checks that the pattern of what it hides says nothing more to someone who knows how SUDS works; a whole period is refused, and nothing published, when even the number of people served cannot be shown safely (a small program's month now and then), or when the check cannot confirm the release even with tables withheld or cannot finish (years and quarters of programmes of many sizes, depending on their months' overdose figures). The message says what may be tried next and whom to tell; a refusal does not affect the submission to your funder.

**Publishing.** Only one kind of run is a **publication release**: all funding sources, for one calendar month, quarter or fiscal year (starting in January, April, July or October) that has **ended**. A supervisor or an administrator prepares one under **Prepare a publication release** (for the range on screen when it is one, or *Last month*, *Last quarter* or a fiscal year); the page then lists what was withheld and why, what to check before publishing, and the review tick box, with **Back to the submission to your funder**. The heading then says *Publication release*, and the report and its files say *Publication release — small cells screened; review before sharing* (the filename ends `publication-screened-review-before-sharing`). Any other run — one funding source, a custom date range, a year to date, or the caseload of someone held to it — is a submission to the funder or an internal report, **not for publication**, and so is its file (the filename ends `exact-counts` or `internal-suppressed`): two reports of different scope or period can be subtracted from each other to reveal a small group, however carefully each one is suppressed. Publish **one standard period per funder cycle** (for example the fiscal year, once its data are complete): a quarter and the year that contains it can still be subtracted from each other. **Who can run what.** Supervisors and administrators can run any report: a custom range, one funding source, a period not yet ended (all *internal, not for publication*), and exact counts. Navigators and clinicians run it as an internal report, always suppressed: of the whole program (from 1.16.0), or of their own caseload if an administrator holds them to it. **Finance and read-only accounts run publication releases only** — the page says so, opens on the last quarter that has ended and offers only the *Periods you can publish*; a link to any other run says why it is refused. Any internal run could be subtracted from a release to reveal someone (August's release minus 1–30 August is whoever was served on the 31st), so internal runs stay with the roles that can already see client records or run the program. Finance's money and staff hours are exact in a publication release and on **Funding & spending** for any period or fund. The same rule applies to the NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page (the settlement report counts the whole program and needs *See the budget*: a navigator or clinician runs it as an internal report, and one held to their caseload only as a publication release, and only while caseloads are not restricted). The program's **own submission to its funder** (a funder's own fund's figures, or the whole program's) is what a supervisor or administrator gets by default: every cell is the true number, the heading says *Submission to your funder — not for publication*, and the exported file says *Exact counts … not for publication or sharing* on its About sheet (a CSV says it in its first rows; the filename ends `exact-counts`). On the Reports page, **Kind of file** beside the NDP log and the settlement report offers the same three: the submission (exact), the submission with small cells suppressed, and a publication release (for a range that can be one). **This report (Excel)** / **CSV** download the report as shown. On the funder report they are under **Which file do I send?**, each with a line saying what it is for: **This report (Excel)** is the submission workbook to send your funder (for a publication release, the file to publish or share once reviewed); **CSV** is the same figures as plain rows, for a funder portal or data system that asks for an upload; **Export everything to Excel** is every program sheet for the period, for your own checking — not for sending.

**Funding source on visits.** A new visit is pre-filled with the worker's default fund (**Settings → Users & permissions → Edit → Default funding source**) or, if they have none, the program's (**Settings → Program → Reporting**); the worker can still choose another or none. A visit logged by someone who is not shown the field (a clinician) is charged to the same default. The funder report's **By funding source** table has a **No funding source** row, and a warning with a link to exactly those visits so each can be edited. Staff hours: **Approved staff hours** is what a county would invoice; **Logged, not yet approved** (draft or submitted time) is shown beside it, with a warning, so time waiting for approval does not read as zero.

**Harm-reduction reporting** (Reports, for the date range chosen). See [docs/compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md](compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md) for every field and what is not yet verified.
* **Naloxone distribution & reversal log (NDP-style)**, Excel or CSV: one row per day, site type and recipient type with kits and doses distributed (doses = kits × *Naloxone doses per kit*, 2 unless changed in Settings), and one row per day, site type and who gave it for reversals reported (overdose events where naloxone was used and the person survived). Kits come from *Naloxone kits given* on visits, including community distribution with no client; the site type is the visit's *Location*. No names or client codes. The layout follows the NDP's reporting fields as SUDS understands them and **is not the official NDP template**: check it against the current template before submitting.
* **Opioid settlement expenditures**, Excel or CSV (budget roles): spending from opioid settlement funds grouped by allowable use (the settlements' Exhibit E core strategies and approved uses) and California High Impact Abatement Activity, with the share that went to High Impact Abatement Activities, pending spending shown apart, and services charged to those funds. Set the category on the fund (**Funding & spending → Edit**; the setup wizard asks it for a main fund that is opioid settlement money), and on an expenditure only when it differs. The category lists need verification against each fund's agreement. When no fund is settlement money, the Reports page says so beside the buttons, and the file carries a row saying so rather than a bare header.

**Publication releases switched off.** An administrator can switch publication releases off (**Settings → Program → Modules → Publication releases**) while the program waits for its own review of the method. Then *Prepare a publication release* disappears, the submission to your funder carries on as before, and a read-only account is told it has nothing to run until releases are switched back on.

**Settlement spending for DHCS or your county.** Beside the settlement report, **DHCS settlement expenditure layout** gives the same spending one row per activity in the fields of DHCS's expenditure reporting form, with the categories, amounts and people served filled in and the narrative columns (which settlement, the activity's name and description, why it meets the HIAA, outcomes) left for you to write; numbers of people of 10 or fewer are written "10 or fewer", as DHCS asks. **Set up the county template** (finance, supervisors, administrators) matches your county's own form: name it and list its columns in order, each filled from one of those fields, blank, or fixed text; **County template** then downloads the rows in the county's columns. Check both against the current forms.

## Settlement outcomes (finance, supervisors, administrators)
**Settlement outcomes** (sidebar, under *Program*; also linked from Reports and Funding & spending) shows, for each opioid settlement fund on its own — a county's, a city's, the state's share — what it spent and what the program recorded of the work charged to it, for the period you choose (*This quarter*, *Year to date*, *Last 12 months*, *Last month* or any range):
* **All settlement funds**: spent, pending approval, and the headline outcomes together.
* **By settlement category**: each Exhibit E category, its funds, what they spent under it, its outcomes, and the cost per headline outcome.
* **Each fund**: its category and High Impact Abatement Activity, what it spent under its own category and under others, the outcomes for its kind of work with the cost per outcome, and the same **by month**. Overdose reversal (naloxone) spending shows kits distributed and reversals reported; harm reduction and syringe services, contacts and supplies; treatment and connections to care, people served, admissions, referrals and people linked to care and to medication for OUD; prevention and training, education sessions, people trained and staff training hours. Planning, research and administration show spending only. The full list is in [docs/compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md](compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md), section 5.
* **Small counts.** Counts of people are hidden as in the funder report — under 11 shows as `<11`, and a figure that would let one be worked out as `suppressed` — for finance as for everyone, and no cost per outcome is shown beside a hidden count. Supervisors, administrators and finance can choose **Counts → Exact counts** for the program's own use (not for sharing). Money, kits, strips, syringes, contacts and hours are always exact. There are no names or client codes on the page or in its files.
* **Download (Excel)** or **CSV** makes the file when you press it (the About sheet says what it is and how it counts); **Print** prints the page with its period and kind of run. SUDS sends it nowhere.
* **What it is not.** SUDS reports what the program records. This is not an official state reporting system or a state template: for DHCS use the settlement expenditure report and its DHCS layout (Reports → Harm-reduction reporting), and check what your agreement or county asks for before quoting a figure as a grant's outcome.
* **Send to the county** (released in 1.18.0; office server only): the card makes a file of the program's figures for the county that funds it — spending and the outcomes above, by fund, by category and in total, with **exact counts, small numbers included**, and nothing about any client (no names, client codes, dates of birth or single events). Type the **county code** the county gave you (its County view › Programs page shows it) and the county's name, and **tick the settlement funds that county pays for**: only they go into the file, and every total in it is over them alone. Nothing is ticked the first time; next time the funds you chose for that county are ticked again. The **period** is the last complete quarter unless you choose another (each quarter is also named as its California fiscal-year quarter; July to September is FY Q1); it is shown beside **Make the county file for …**, a period that is not a quarter is warned about, and a period that has not ended yet is refused. The server signs the file, so the county can check it came from you unchanged. **Show the key for the county** shows this server's **key fingerprint** and **public key** (each with a Copy button) without making a file: give the county the public key once and read the fingerprint out to them. **Make a new key** retires the key and shows the new fingerprint to read to the county, which must add it before it imports files signed with it. The file **leaves the program**: it is for the county under your funding contract, not for publication; making it is recorded in the audit log, and SUDS sends nothing itself — send the file the way the county asks. Where the county runs SUDS and has switched on the **county connection**, an administrator can connect this server to it (**Send to the county over the connection › Connect to the county**: the county's address and the token it issued); then **Test connection** shows which periods the county still expects, and **Send to the county now** sends the same file the download makes, for the funds ticked above, and shows the county's receipt, with a log of what was sent (docs/COUNTY-VIEW.md, *Connecting*). Finance, supervisors and administrators (whoever files the funder submission). Not on SUDS on this device.

## County view (for a county that funds programs)
Released in 1.18.0; office server only. A county that runs SUDS sees the settlement spending and outcomes of the programs it funds, from the signed files they send (above), side by side and added up. Administrators, supervisors and finance can see it; administrators register programs and import files (see `docs/COUNTY-VIEW.md`).
* **Programs** (*County view → Programs*): **this county's code** (with a Copy button) to give each program. **Register a program**: its name and the public key it sent. SUDS shows the key's fingerprint as you paste it: type the fingerprint the program reads out (SUDS refuses a key that does not match), or tick that you compared the two. **Keys** shows a program's key history: **Replace the key** when it makes a new one (its files signed with the old key keep counting, unless you tick that the old key was compromised); **Old key compromised** stops counting what an old key signed, and **Trust again** undoes it. **Edit** renames a program, adds notes or deactivates it: an inactive program's new files are refused and its files stop counting, unless you tick *While inactive, keep counting the files it already sent*.
* **Submissions** (*County view → Submissions*): choose the file and **Import**. SUDS checks it was made for this county, signed with that program's current key and not changed since, that it is a county submission and that its period has ended, and says what happened where you are (the result takes the focus). Of two files for the same program and period, the one **made** later counts, whichever arrived first; the other is kept as *Replaced*. The same file twice changes nothing. **Withdraw** (with a reason, kept in the audit log) takes a current file out of the figures and keeps it; if it had replaced an earlier file, that one counts again. **Reinstate** puts a withdrawn file back. Each file shows as *Current*, *Replaced*, *Withdrawn* or *Not counted: key compromised*.
* **Combined view**: choose a period (a preset — last quarter, the fiscal year so far or the last one and their quarters, last calendar year, year to date — or any dates; Enter applies them) and whether to show the whole period or **by quarter**. The headline says how many programs submitted for the whole period, part of it and not at all; *Who has submitted* lists each program with the files that count and when they were received. Then spending (all settlement funds, by Exhibit E allowable use, by High Impact Abatement Activity) and each outcome, one column per program and **Total (N of M programs complete)**; a program with nothing for the period shows "— not submitted". **Download (Excel, internal)**, **CSV**, or the **long CSV, one row per figure** for your own analysis.
* **County connections** (optional, off by default): an administrator who also manages settings can switch on the county connection (*County connections*), so programs that run SUDS send their files straight to this server. **Programs → Connection tokens → Issue token** makes one program's token, shown once to give it; the file it sends must still be signed with that program's current key and made for this county. **Issue a read token** is for the county's own systems to read the combined view (docs/COUNTY-VIEW.md, *Connecting*).
* **Programs not on SUDS** (released in 1.20.0; administrators): **Programs → Add a program not on SUDS** registers a program that does not run SUDS (a name, no key). Its **Enter figures** takes the period, the **source document** the figures come from ("Q2 report emailed 3 July 2026") and, for each fund, the same figures a signed file carries; **Import a CSV** takes the long CSV's layout (**Download a template for this program** gives one), and **Check the file** says what is wrong, by row and column, before anything is saved. The figures are marked *Entered by the county — not signed by the program* everywhere, a signed file from the program for the same period always counts instead, and **Leave out figures entered by the county** on the combined view takes them away. **Correct** reopens the form; **Withdraw** and **Reinstate** work as for files. Once the program starts running SUDS and you add its key, its entered figures can be withdrawn or reinstated but not corrected (docs/COUNTY-VIEW.md, *County-entered figures*).
* **Which files count.** Only a file whose whole period lies inside the period you chose; nothing is pro-rated. If a program sent a quarter and a month inside it, the quarter counts.
* **Read the caveats.** People are counted by each program and added up: someone served by two programs counts twice — the figures are *each program's own count, summed*, and the county view can never remove that double counting. The counts are exact, for authorised county staff only, and not for publication: publishing them needs the publication screen over the combined release, which is planned.

## SUPRT-A (State Opioid Response client-level reporting)
Shown when the program has a **SOR grant** funding source (or an administrator switches the module on). A client who receives a service charged to the SOR grant needs a **baseline**; then a **reassessment** at 6 months (3 if your contract says so), an **annual assessment** each year, and a **closeout** when services end. Each reassessment and annual assessment is collected in a window from 30 days before to 30 days after the anniversary. The client's **SUPRT-A** tab shows what is due and opens the form: what SUDS already knows (the client code, substance, MAT, overdoses, screenings, diagnoses, services since the last assessment, demographics) is filled in and says so; change anything that is not right and ask the rest. Record whether the client completed SAMHSA's own questionnaire (SUPRT-C), which SUDS does not hold. Follow-ups due are on your **To-dos** page (a supervisor sees the team's). The **SUPRT-A** page (Reports) shows completion rates and, for a supervisor, makes the **file for entry into SPARS**: it names clients, so it asks to whom and on what basis, leaves out anyone without a consent naming the recipient, and is written to each client's accounting of disclosures. Check it against the current SUPRT handbook before entering it; see [docs/compliance/SUPRT.md](compliance/SUPRT.md).

## State reporting (CalOMS Tx) and the county EHR hand-off
**Reports → State reporting** (shown when the program uses the CalOMS Tx or county EHR hand-off module). A treatment program that must report CalOMS Tx has an administrator turn it on there (tick *This program reports CalOMS Tx* and enter the program's CalOMS provider ID). From then on, **Start an episode** on a client's Episodes tab asks the CalOMS admission questions, and **Discharge** asks the CalOMS discharge status and date of last service (and the past-30-day questions, unless the discharge is administrative). The **CalOMS records** button on each episode shows what it has, any problems, and what is still needed — for example an admission record for an episode opened at intake, or an annual update on each anniversary of admission. Prevention and navigation programs leave it off and are never asked.

The **Validation report** lists every problem in the period by client code and field (fatal errors first); fix them from the client's Episodes tab. A supervisor can **Download preview** — a zip of CSV files plus the monthly provider activity report, marked *PREVIEW - NOT FOR SUBMISSION*, with no names or dates of birth — to check it. When the records are ready, **Produce submission file**: that is the disclosure, so each client in it gets a *State reporting (CalOMS)* entry in their accounting of disclosures and its records show as sent, and the file downloads. Send that file, unchanged, through the county's CalOMS process; it is listed under *Submissions produced* (with its SHA-256) and can be downloaded again for 90 days. Records that still have fatal errors are held back. The code values are verified against the DHCS data dictionary (v3.0, Oct 2024); the column names and file layout are SUDS's own (the provider activity report's *NoActivity* column, `Y`/`N`, too: the dictionary does not cover that report) — read `docs/compliance/CALOMS.md` before the first submission.

**After upgrading from 1.24 or earlier: re-ask old admissions.** Since 1.25.0 every CalOMS admission must answer four questions the state's data dictionary requires: medication prescribed as part of treatment, criminal justice status, sexual orientation, and consent for future contact. Admissions recorded before 1.25.0 do not have them, so after the upgrade each one shows several fatal problems ("… is required") on the validation report and the worklist, and is held back from the submission file with the discharges and annual updates that follow it. Answers that no longer fit (for example a "Yes" to a question that is now a number of days) are listed the same way. Before the next monthly file, ask each client with an open episode and record the answers with **CalOMS records** on the client's Episodes tab. A race recorded twice by the upgrade (Other Pacific Islander and Other both became Other Race) counts as one answer and needs nothing.

**Monthly preparation, the worklist and the submission log (1.17.0).** An administrator can set a **Monthly run** (State reporting → Settings: a day of the month, 1–28, and optionally *One file per provider ID*). On that day SUDS checks the previous month against every edit check and **prepares** a submission file for the records that pass. A prepared file is **not** a disclosure: nothing is accounted, no record is marked as sent, and it cannot be downloaded. *Check and prepare (not sent)* does the same for the period on screen. Someone allowed identified exports then **Produces** it, which is the disclosure (exactly as producing a file by hand) and downloads it; if any record in it changed after it was prepared, producing is refused — **Discard** it and prepare again. **To fix before the next submission** is the worklist: every validation problem, assigned to the record's owner (*Mine* first for front-line staff). Each submission's **Log** shows who prepared, produced, downloaded or discarded it, and **Record upload** keeps the date you uploaded it to DHCS and the reference the portal gave. SUDS still never uploads to DHCS and holds no DHCS login. Each CalOMS provider ID can carry its legal name and NPI, and a file can be made for one provider; several unrelated organisations on one server is not supported.

**SUDS does not submit Drug Medi-Cal claims.** Where a service must be billed, the **County EHR hand-off** on the same page produces the encounters (one row per client, per day, per kind of service and worker, with minutes, place and funding source) for entry into the county EHR. It includes names and Medi-Cal IDs, so it needs a recipient, a purpose and a lawful basis; with the consent basis, clients with no consent on file naming that recipient are left out and listed. It is never made on an "other" basis: a supervisor's justified override covers one client's disclosure, not a whole file. Identified exports on Reports work the same way; an *internal* export may only go to this program or its staff, and a QSOA, research or audit export needs the agreement or approval with the recipient on file. See `docs/SCOPE.md`.

## Importing spreadsheets
Import → *Import a spreadsheet or EHR export*. Choose what you are importing (clients, resources, visits, calls, time, to-dos, expenditures), download the template or upload the spreadsheet you already keep. SUDS matches your column names automatically (you can adjust them), checks every row, tells you exactly what is wrong with any row, flags people who already exist, and only saves when you click Import. For visits, calls and other client records, refer to the client by code (C26-0012) or "Last, First".

## Working faster
* **Keyboard shortcuts.** **/** puts the cursor in the search box at the top, **n** opens *Log a visit*, **?** lists the shortcuts, and **Ctrl + Enter** (**⌘ + Enter** on a Mac) saves the form that is open, exactly as its Save button would. The single keys work only when you are not typing in a field and no dialog is open. If they get in your way (for example with speech recognition, which can send a single letter), switch them off under **My profile → Keyboard shortcuts**; Ctrl + Enter keeps working. This is the setting WCAG 2.1.4 asks for.
* **Undo.** Deactivating a program in the resource directory, retiring a policy or contract, and ending someone's assignment to a client's care team happen at once, with an **Undo** toast for 10 seconds (it stays while the pointer is over it or its button has the keyboard, and it is read out by screen readers). Undo puts the program back in the pickers, the document back in effect, or the same worker back on the case in the same role (a new assignment from today; the ended one stays in the history). Both the action and the undo are in the audit log. Anything that cannot be taken back — deleting a record, signing a note, deactivating a staff account — still asks first.
* **Nothing here yet?** An empty list says what to do next, with the button that does it when you may; when you may not, it says who does (for example: "An administrator or supervisor uploads the program's policies and contracts").
* **The ? beside a heading** explains it. The Part 2 consent form's **Is this consent a valid basis for sharing?** says which consent covers what (TPO, a single recipient, counseling notes, legal proceedings) and what never counts (a general release, a verbal OK, one that has expired or been revoked). **Adjust stock**, **Dispose of** and **Move stock** on Supplies each say when to use which: dispose of stock you are throwing away, move stock between your sites, adjust only when the count is wrong — and never adjust for kits handed out (log the visit). The funder report's **Which file do I send?** says which download goes to the funder.
