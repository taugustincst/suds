# Letter: a harm-reduction pilot for a county's opioid-settlement coordinator

**For the owner (or the business assistant on the owner's instruction).** A first letter to the person who
administers a county's opioid-settlement funds, proposing a 90-day pilot of SUDS with one to three harm-reduction
programmes the county already funds. The pilot's structure, roles and success measures are in
[../COUNTY-KIT.md](../COUNTY-KIT.md); how SUDS supports the DHCS settlement expenditure report is in
[OSF-REPORTING-ONE-PAGER.md](OSF-REPORTING-ONE-PAGER.md). Fill the bracketed items; delete this paragraph.

**Before you send it**, check the *Honest status* paragraph against
[../../security/OPERATIVE-CONTROLS.md](../../security/OPERATIVE-CONTROLS.md) and the business work plan
([../BUSINESS-WORKPLAN.md](../BUSINESS-WORKPLAN.md)): insurance, counsel's review of the agreements and the
independent pen test change what it may say. A county reviewer will compare this letter with the security
questionnaire; say nothing here that the questionnaire does not.

**Why this buyer.** The county holds settlement money that must be spent and accounted for, files the DHCS expenditure
report each year by 30 September (which now asks about prior-year funds left unspent), and needs outcome evidence from
the community organisations it funds. Those organisations mostly record their work in spreadsheets, REDCap or monthly
forms. A county sponsor can pay for a pilot from its settlement allocation (for example under Exhibit E's data
collection, or within the administrative share the county allows) — **the county's counsel decides which category
applies; never state it as settled.**

Subject: A 90-day pilot: settlement outcomes from your harm-reduction grantees, without extra reporting work

Dear [name],

[County] funds [one to three named programmes] with opioid-settlement money, and reports to DHCS each year on how it
was spent. I'd like to propose a 90-day pilot that gives you those programmes' outcomes by settlement fund — contacts,
naloxone kits, test strips, overdose reversals, people served and linked to care — without asking their staff to fill
in another form.

**What it is.** SUDS is case-management and reporting software for harm-reduction and SUD programmes, made in
California by AugustInnovations LLC. Outreach workers log contacts on a phone (named or anonymous, offline if the
programme allows), supplies are drawn down by site and lot, and each programme can send the county a signed file of
its outcomes and spending for the funds the county pays for. The county sees every funded programme in one view, with
no client records.

**The pilot.** Ninety days with [one to three] programmes, on fictional data first and real records only after the
agreements are signed. Each programme runs SUDS on its own office server (or, for a very small team, free in a browser
on one device). At day 90 you and the programmes decide whether to continue, with measures agreed at the start (the
pilot plan is attached).

**Cost.** $14,500 per programme for the pilot, credited in full toward the first year; after that $24,500 a year per
programme (up to 30 staff) or $49,500 for up to five sites. A county-wide arrangement is $98,000 a year. The
published prices are at sudsdata.com.
