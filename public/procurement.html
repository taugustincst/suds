What SUDS is

SUDS records the work of harm-reduction, outreach and prevention programmes: outreach contacts, visits, naloxone and supply distribution, referrals and follow-ups, and the reports their funders ask for, with HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 privacy controls. Clinical modules are optional. It is not an electronic health record and does not bill. The software is open source under the MIT licence.

Two ways to run it

On an office server. A programme (or its IT partner or county) installs SUDS on a server it controls. Staff use it in the browser on a computer, phone or tablet; the office server is the system of record, with scheduled encrypted backups. Sign up asks for an account, which an administrator approves.

SUDS on this device. The same web application, published as a website, for a person or a small team with no office server. Records are kept only in that browser on that device, encrypted, and protected by the backups its users download. Nothing is sent anywhere and it does not sync with an office server.

Either way it is the same production application; the platform policy explains the difference.

Independent testing and attestation

Penetration test: not yet commissioned. No independent penetration test of SUDS has been done. A test scope is ready, written so a county can commission one against its own staging installation.

SUDS holds no SOC 2, ISO 27001, HITRUST, StateRAMP or FedRAMP attestation. The threat model and questionnaire are the project's own work, and each answer names the code, test or document that shows it.
