# SUDS user guide (navigators, clinicians, supervisors)

## The three things to know
1. **Home** shows what needs attention today and where you left off — on any device.
2. **+ Log** (blue button; bottom-right on a phone) logs a visit, an overdose or reversal, a call, a text, a note, a to-do, a referral or time.
3. The **search box** at the top finds a client by last name, phone or code. Open a client to see their whole story.

Every page has a **?** that explains it in plain language. Records are never truly deleted, so you cannot break anything. The first time you sign in, a **Welcome to SUDS** card at the top of Home says the same in a few lines; **Got it** puts it away, and **Help** at the foot of the menu brings it back. It never stands in front of the page: you can log a visit straight away.

**Two words used everywhere.** A **visit** is any service you provide — face to face or by phone: outreach, a screening, a warm handoff, a naloxone kit, a ride (reports and exports call them *Visits*). A **to-do** is a follow-up or reminder, yours or one a visit or call created for you (the **To-dos** page, the client's *To-dos* tab, **+ To-do**, and **To-dos for today** on Home). The buttons say the same everywhere: **Log a visit** and **Make a referral**.

## Your sidebar
The sidebar shows the pages your role uses every day, most used first. For navigators and clinicians that is Home, Clients, Waitlist, To-dos, Visits, Calls & texts, Notes, Supplies, Overdose & reversals, Referrals and the Resource directory; **More** at the bottom opens the rest you may use (Forms, My time, Reports, the Funder report — for your own caseload, as an internal run — State reporting and SUPRT-A when your program uses them, Privacy & Part 2, Import). **Supervisors** see Home, then **Supervision** (the work waiting on them), then Clients, Waitlist, To-dos, Visits, Calls & texts, Notes, Referrals and **Settings** (moving a caseload and the audit log); **More** holds the rest they may use: Supplies, Overdose & reversals, Forms, My time, the Resource directory, Reports, the Funder report, State reporting and SUPRT-A when the program uses them, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2 and Import. Finance and administrators also see the **Program** section — Reports, Funder report, State reporting (CalOMS Tx and the county EHR hand-off) and SUPRT-A when those modules are on, Funding & spending, Policies & contracts, Privacy & Part 2, Import and Settings. On a phone the top bar's **Clients** opens the client list. Nothing is taken away from anyone: a page that is not in your sidebar still opens from a link or its address if your role may use it, and client assistance (bus passes, IDs) is recorded on the client's *Assistance $* tab.

## Program profile and modules
An administrator chooses what kind of program this is in the setup wizard, and can change it at any time under **Settings → Program → Program profile & modules**:
* **Harm reduction & outreach** (the choice for a new install): outreach and visits, supplies, calls and texts, referrals, and grant reporting. The clinical modules are hidden.
* **Treatment-adjacent**: all of that plus the clinical modules — **care plan & problem list**, **assessments** (six-dimension ASAM-aligned assessments and scored screenings), **CalOMS Tx** state reporting, the **FHIR API** and the **county EHR hand-off**.

Each module can also be switched on (or off) by itself, whatever the profile — a harm-reduction program that keeps a care plan switches on just that one. A module that is off leaves the menus, the client's tabs, the Overview and Reports, and SUDS refuses new records in it; anything recorded while it was on can still be read, and permissions do not change. A SUDS that was upgraded from an earlier version keeps everything it was using: it starts as treatment-adjacent if it already had any care plan, assessment, CalOMS or FHIR set-up, and as harm reduction otherwise. Devices that sync with the office show what the office shows.

## Phone, tablet and computer stay in sync
Use SUDS wherever you are: on the office computer, or in the browser on your phone or tablet at **https://suds.local** (office Wi-Fi). There is no app to install: open the address, sign in, and add it to your home screen once — iPhone: Safari **Share → Add to Home Screen**; Android: browser menu **⋮ → Install app**. The `/app` page (**https://suds.local/app**) shows these steps. Your clients, notes, reminders and even half-written note drafts are the same everywhere, because every device talks to the same office SUDS.

SUDS needs a connection to the office. Without one, a banner says so and nothing can be saved until you reconnect. If your program has approved an offline copy for field work (local mode), your administrator will set it up with you; the office SUDS is always the master copy, and anything it refuses on sync is final.

## Signing in
Use your individual username. After 5 wrong passwords the account locks for 15 minutes. You are signed out automatically after 15 minutes without activity — a banner warns you one minute before. Supervisors and administrators must enroll an authenticator app (Profile → Multi-factor authentication → scan the QR code).

**Forgot your password or locked out?** On the office SUDS, ask your supervisor or a SUDS administrator to reset it (Settings → Users). If the administrator is the one locked out, whoever runs the SUDS server resets that account on the server itself (`npm run reset-admin -- <username>`, docs/INSTALL.md).

**On SUDS on this device** the records are encrypted with your password, so nobody can simply reset it for you. When the device is set up, SUDS shows a **recovery code** once: download it or print it, keep it somewhere safe away from the device (whoever has it can open every record on the device, like a key), and tick *I have saved my recovery code*. The sign-in page's **Can't sign in?** section then offers three ways back in:

* **Use your recovery code** — type the code and a new password. You are signed in with every record kept, and SUDS shows a new code to save instead (the one you used stops working). Only the person who manages the device gets back in this way.
* **Restore from a backup** — puts a backup file back, with the accounts and passwords it had. Anything recorded since the backup is lost.
* **Start over on this device** — erases every record in this browser. Nothing is kept.

The person who manages the device can make a new recovery code at any time under **This device → Recovery code** (it asks for their password first; the old code stops working). A device set up before this was added has no code: Home asks its manager to make one until they do.

## Dashboard
Shows alerts (for supervisors and administrators, the team's overdue to-dos; your own are counted on the 🔔 bell in the header and listed under **To-dos for today**; unsigned notes, imported notes waiting for review, clients with no visit in 30 days, consents expiring, open client rights requests (access, amendment, restriction, an accounting of disclosures) and how many are past their 30-day deadline, active clients with no Part 2 notice on record, and — for supervisors — open privacy complaints and incidents nearing or past their notification deadline), your caseload sorted by risk and last contact, and 90-day activity charts. The visits and naloxone kits counted there include outreach and community distribution recorded with no client, and kits handed out on any kind of visit (the **Naloxone kits given** tile opens the visits that handed some out): a navigator's figures are their caseload's visits and the anonymous outreach they logged themselves; a supervisor's, an administrator's, finance's and read-only's are the whole program's, and agree with the funder report for the same period (the activity card is headed *What the team has been doing* for supervisors and *The program's visits* for finance and read-only). The budget figure (spent of budget) is shown only to the roles that approve spending — supervisors, finance and administrators. The same open-requests count appears on the Supervision page.

A **read-only account** (one that can look but not record anything) has a Home that starts with **Your reports** — the Funder report, Reports and the Resource directory — above the program's figures, and no *To-dos for today* or *Continue where you left off* card: it keeps no to-dos and opens no client records. Which cards Home shows follows the permissions an account actually has, including any an administrator has granted or denied it individually.

For **finance and read-only accounts**, Home and Reports show a count of people from 1 to 10 as **<11** (the same small-cell threshold as the funder report; read-only runs it as publication releases only, finance also as the program's submission to its funder); visits, calls, naloxone kits, hours and money stay exact. **Visits by worker** — a named staff member's activity — is shown only to supervisors and administrators. On a device copy there is no "also signed in on another computer" line: its sign-ins are all on that device.

**Your first day.** The welcome card on Home (until you press *Got it*; **Help** at the foot of the menu brings it back) holds three first steps for your role — for a navigator: log a visit, find a client, check your to-dos; a clinician: open the caseload, write a clinical note, look over unsigned notes; a supervisor: Supervision, the caseload, the funder report; finance: funds, time to approve, the funder report; read-only: the funder report, reports, the resource directory. Each has the button that does it; using it, or ticking its box, marks it done. An administrator has **Finish setting up** instead.

**The 🔔 bell** counts your to-dos due within the hour or overdue. Press it to see them, with when the list was last updated and **Refresh**. It asks again every minute, a moment after you save anything, and when you come back to the window.

**Finish setting up** (administrators) lists what a new office server still needs: backups, two-step verification, the key backup, staff, the consent form, the resource directory, funding sources — and, once there are funds, **a default fund** (Settings › Program › Reporting) if none is set, and **each opioid settlement fund's category** when the settlement report would list its spending as *Uncategorised*. It also offers **Save a usual consent naming your referral partners**: the program's usual consent, which workers fill a new consent in from in one step. You tick the partners it names from the resource directory (nothing is ticked for you, and crisis lines are not offered: they are numbers a client is given, not organisations that receive records); they are added after the consent's wording, and are ticked again when you reopen it. And **Add your supplies**, while the Supplies cupboard is empty: one click adds *Naloxone kit* and *Fentanyl test strips* (with nothing on hand yet; record a stock-take under Supplies). Visits take kits and strips off those two items only once they exist; until then the visit form says so before it is saved (and offers a supervisor or administrator the same one click), and Supplies offers to add them.

**Security findings** on a production server (administrators) are shown above the rest of Home as a sentence saying what is wrong and who fixes it, with a link to Security status — amber for a finding such as the audit log's safety copies (anchors) being on the same disk as the database, red when an audit check has actually failed or backups are off. They cannot be dismissed; they go when the configuration is fixed. **Load sample data** is offered on Home only on a server not in production mode (and on a device); a production server keeps it under Settings › Program › Sample data.

**Two-step verification.** If your role needs it and you have not set it up, a one-line bar says by when. Dismiss it (✕) and it becomes a small **🔐 2-step** link in the header, on every device, until you set it up.

## Clients
* **Search** matches exact last name, "Last, First", phone number, date of birth (YYYY-MM-DD) or client code, plus the first few letters of a surname and (for names in the Latin alphabet) close misspellings. Either part of a double surname finds the person: *Vasquez* (or *Vas*) finds *Quintero-Vasquez*. When you choose a client in a form, the list opens only once you type, and closes when you pick someone or move on. Names in any script work — Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese and so on — and accents do not matter: *Oster* finds *Øster*, *Lecki* finds *Łecki*, *Jose* finds *José*. A name search lists the best matches first: the exact name, then the clients you have worked with lately, then names that start the same way, then look-alike spellings. The search box at the top of every page leaves the look-alikes out when anything matched better (a misspelling with no better match still finds them), also lists matching programs from the resource directory, and says what each result is (*Client* or *Resource*). Press **/** to jump to it (see *Keyboard shortcuts*).
* **Consent running out.** When one of a client's consents expires within 30 days, the top of their **Overview** says which one and on what date, with a link to the **Consents** tab to record a renewed one. A consent that has already expired while an open referral still relies on it is named there too.
* **New client** — opens **Quick add**: the first and last name (the only required fields), the name they go by, and a date of birth or phone if they give one (with the last name, those find an earlier record of the same person). **Create client** creates the record and opens it; **Add details** there carries on with the rest of the intake whenever you have it, in the same sections. **Full intake** in Quick add opens the whole intake form instead, with what you typed carried over (use it to set status *waitlist* for someone not yet enrolled). On the intake, **Who referred them to us** is how the person came to the program; the referrals you make to other providers are under **Referrals**.
* The client page shows six tabs, in the order a visit is worked, on every screen size: Overview, Visits, Notes, To-dos, Consents and Referrals — and, beside them, what your own work turns to most: **Care plan** and **Assessments** for clinicians, **Episodes** and **Care team** for supervisors (when the program uses those modules, and while there is room on the screen; at a very large text size some move under More). **More** holds the rest: Calls, Problems, Care plan, Assessments (clinicians and supervisors), SUPRT-A, Forms, Episodes, Requests, Time, Assistance $ (for roles that see the budget) and Care team. Problems, Care plan, Assessments and SUPRT-A appear when the program uses those modules and the client has something in them or you can add to them (see *Program profile and modules*). The **Overview** ends with **Recent activity** — the latest visits, calls, notes, referrals and to-dos in date order — and **All activity** there opens the whole history (it was the Timeline tab). See *Problem list, care plan and assessments* below.
* The buttons at the top of a client are **+ Log a visit**, **↻ Repeat last visit**, **+ Call**, **+ Text**, **+ Note**, **+ To-do** and **+ Make a referral** (the referral form opens for that client, with their consents). **On a phone** they fold into one **Add…** button that opens the same list.
* **Requests** records patient-rights requests (access, amendment, restriction, accounting of disclosures), each due 30 days after it was received. Mark one *Fulfilled* or *Denied* when answered; **Edit** corrects its kind, dates or notes (or reopens it); **Delete** is only for a request recorded in error — a request you are refusing is marked *Denied* so it stays on file. Edits and deletions are in the audit log.
* **Safety flags** (e.g. "no home visits alone") appear as a red badge on every page for that client.
* **Status and episodes.** Starting an episode of care makes the client *active* (including someone taken off the waitlist). While an episode is open, *Closed* and *Deceased* cannot be chosen on the Edit form: discharge on the Episodes tab instead — that closes the episode, ends the care team and clears open to-dos. The discharge dialog has no default reason; choose one.
* **After a discharge**, a navigator whose only link to the client was their assignment loses access to the record the next day (the discharge ends every assignment). If you need to look back at a discharged client — to answer a records request, or when they return — ask a supervisor to assign you again, or to look it up for you. Supervisors and administrators see every record.
* **Possible duplicates at intake.** While you type a new client's name, date of birth or phone, SUDS shows any matching record you can already open, and offers to re-admit a discharged earlier record of the same person when the surname and date of birth match (a phone number alone is never enough). The offer says only that an earlier record exists: not its client code, nor when or why the person was discharged, and a supervisor gets a task to review it. **Re-admit this person** asks why right there, under the offer (at least 8 characters, for example "walked in" — it is for the supervisor who reviews it), in the same dialog. The check is limited to 60 in 15 minutes per person. A match on someone else's caseload is never shown or counted to you (that would say the person is a client here): your intake goes ahead as usual and a supervisor gets a high-priority task on the existing record to compare the two and merge them if they are the same person. A new client entered on a phone or tablet is checked the same way when it syncs.
* **Duplicates.** Merge a duplicate from the Care team tab. The record merged away is kept, marked as merged, and an old link to it opens the record it was merged into. A record on **legal hold** cannot be merged, in either direction, until an administrator clears the hold.
* **Legal hold** can only be placed or cleared by an administrator (it usually comes from counsel). A supervisor who needs one placed should ask the administrator; the hold, its reason and who set it are in the audit log.
* **Contact details** are checked when saved: a date of birth cannot be in the future or before 1900, an email must look like one, and a phone number needs at least seven digits.

## Logging work
* **+ Log a visit** (on a client, on the Visits page, or **+ Log → Log a visit**) — the core service record, as a field note. The form shows what every visit has: who (optional for **outreach** and **naloxone distribution** — a kit handed to someone who gives no name counts in the funder report's community distribution; every other type needs the client), what you did (outreach, SBIRT screening, warm handoff, naloxone distribution, post-overdose follow-up…), when, the **supplies given** (naloxone kits and fentanyl test strips roll up into reports and update the client's naloxone status) and a short summary — seven fields at most on a phone, for any kind of visit. The rest is in sections you open when a visit needs them: **Where & how** (location and modality, filled in from your last visit; the folded heading says what they are, for example "Office · In person"), **Outcome & follow-up** (a follow-up date creates a to-do), **Syringe services**, **Funding & cost**, **Time** (duration and the time entry), **Recorded by** (supervisors) and **Add a note**. A section opens by itself when it holds something (an edit with a cost, say) or a field in it needs fixing, and otherwise stays as you last left it; a folded one says what it holds ("adds 30 min of direct service to your time").
* **An unsent visit is kept.** Everything typed into a new visit — every field, the supplies and a note written with it — is kept while the page stays open, if the dialog is closed by accident, you go to another page or you are signed out for inactivity. Opening **Log a visit** again asks **Resume your unsent visit?** (*Resume* puts it all back, *Discard* drops it), and after signing back in the same question is at the top of the page. It is kept in the page's memory only, never in the browser's storage, so reloading the page or closing the tab loses it; and it is yours only: if someone else signs in on that screen, it is gone, never shown to them.
* **The same visit twice?** Saving a new visit for a client who already has one of the same kind that day asks **Save another visit?** first, saying when it was recorded and by whom. **Save another** saves it (two visits in a day happen); **Cancel** goes back to the form with nothing saved. A new visit starts with your last visit's type, location and modality (the program's default location — **Street / Outdoor** for a harm-reduction program, **Office** for a treatment-adjacent one — until you have one).
* **Time from a visit.** *Also log this as a time entry* adds the visit's duration to your time (My time) as a draft to submit. It starts **unticked on every visit** (since 1.15.3; it used to start ticked, and a duration nobody checked went on time sheets): tick it when the visit should be on your time sheet, and it says how many minutes it adds. If the duration is still the 30 minutes the form starts with, Save asks **Log this time?** first — **Log 30 minutes**, or **Change the duration**, which takes you back to it. Supervisors approve hours from these entries, so tick it whenever the visit was paid time. A call works the same way (*Also log as time entry*, unticked). **Log time** by hand for a day (and client) that a visit or call already logged time for says so while you fill it in, and asks **Log this time as well?** before saving, so the same work is not counted twice (*Log it anyway* saves it: a second session with the same person is real time). The time list marks entries *(from visit)* and *(from call)*.
* **A note with the visit.** The short summary holds no names or health details. **Add a note** in the visit form writes the note there and then: the same kinds, formats and Part 2 flag as **+ Note**, saved with the visit in one step as a draft on the client's record, linked to the visit (sign it from Notes). It needs a client.
* **Supplies the program has no item for.** If Supplies keeps no naloxone kit or fentanyl test strip item, the visit form says so before you save — they count on the visit and in reports but are not taken off any stock — and offers a supervisor or administrator **Add naloxone kits and test strips** right there (everyone else is told to ask one). Kits are always entered in the one *Supplies given* list; the two plain number fields appear only when the program keeps no supply items at all.
* **+ Call** — inbound/outbound, who, duration, outcome (reached, voicemail…), crisis flag, encrypted summary, follow-up. *Also log as time entry* starts unticked, as on a visit.
* **Overdose & reversals** (sidebar) — record every overdose and naloxone reversal, including community ones with no client. *Where* starts where the program's visits do, and never offers Phone or Telehealth; *Funding source* starts on your default fund, as a visit's does; *Doses given* can be left empty, and the list then says *given, doses not recorded* (never a dose count nobody entered), the same 0 the funder report and the NDP log count. A message confirms the event was recorded, with the doses. **+ Log → Overdose or reversal** opens the same form from anywhere. Recording a **fatal** overdose for a client discharges them as deceased: after you confirm, their open episode is closed with the reason *deceased*, the care team ends and open to-dos are cancelled. If that was a mistake, change the outcome or **Delete** the event (in the event's dialog): the client goes back to the status they had, the episode is reopened, and the care team and to-dos are restored.
* **Time tracking** — non-client time (documentation, travel, meetings, training) is logged here; choose the funding source when the grant requires effort reporting.
* **To-dos** — follow-ups and reminders (**+ To-do** on a client, **+ Add a to-do** on the To-dos page); mark milestones (★) to show them in the client's recent activity (Overview) and **All activity**.

## Problem list, care plan and assessments (CalAIM)
These are the **care plan & problem list** and **assessments** modules: they are shown when the program is treatment-adjacent or an administrator has switched them on (see *Program profile and modules*). They follow the CalAIM Behavioral Health Documentation Redesign: a current **problem list**, **progress notes** that say which problems they address, a **care coordination plan**, and assessment by the six **ASAM dimensions** (SUDS's six-dimension assessment is ASAM-aligned; it does not include the ASAM Criteria). How each requirement maps to SUDS, and what is out of scope, is in `docs/compliance/CALAIM.md`.

* **Overview → Clinical picture** sums it up: active problems, how many care plan goals are active and any review that is overdue (in red), the latest six-dimension assessment (the six ratings and the level of care), and each outcome measure's latest score with its trend. A PHQ-9 whose question 9 was answered above "Not at all" shows a red safety banner at the top. Each part appears only for roles that may see it.
* **Problems** — the client's current problems and needs, in plain words. **+ Problem** adds one, optionally with an **ICD-10-CM code** (typed in; SUDS checks the format — a letter, two characters, then optionally a dot and up to four more — and tidies it, so *f1120* becomes *F11.20*; there is no code look-up) and **social determinant (Z) codes** (open *Social determinants* to tick common Z55–Z65 codes such as Z59.02 Unsheltered homelessness, or type others). Set where it came from (self-report, assessment, referral) and when it started. Problems are never deleted: **Resolve** one (it is dated today) or make it *inactive*; **History** shows every change — who, when, and the old and new values. Everyone on the care team can keep the list; an administrator can read it.
* **Notes → Problems this note addresses.** The note form lists the client's active problems; tick the ones the service was about. The note shows them under *Addresses*, and the Problems tab counts the notes for each.
* **Care plan** — **+ Goal** records a goal *in the client's own words*, tied to a problem, with a target date and a **review date**. Once the review date passes, the goal (and the Overview) says *Review overdue*; **Reviewed** records that you went over it with the client today and sets the next review. **+ Step** adds what will be done, by whom (client, staff, family/support, another provider) and by when; tick *Also add it to the to-do list* to put a staff step on someone's to-do list, and marking the step **Done** closes that to-do. Mark a goal *Met*, *Partially met*, *Not met* or *Discontinued* rather than deleting it (delete is only for a goal entered in error, by whoever added it or a supervisor). **Print** produces a one-page plan with the active problems, goals and steps, and lines for signatures.
* **Assessments** (clinicians and supervisors) — **+ Six-dimension assessment** (ASAM-aligned) records a 0–4 risk rating for each of the six dimensions with what supports it, the **recommended** level of care, the level **referred to**, and — required when they differ — why (waitlist, client preference, not available…). SUDS records the ratings and the decision; use your program's own licensed ASAM Criteria materials for the rating definitions. SUDS does not include the ASAM Criteria; "ASAM" is a trademark of the American Society of Addiction Medicine, and a program that uses the Criteria needs its own licence from ASAM. The latest assessment sets the client's *ASAM level* field and is shown on the Overview; earlier ones stay in the history.
* **Outcome measures** — **+ PHQ-9**, **+ GAD-7**, **+ AUDIT-C** and **+ Wellbeing (0–10)** open the questionnaire. **+ DAST-10** appears only once an administrator has turned it on under **Settings → Screening instruments**, confirming that the program holds the rights to use it (its licence covers non-commercial clinical use with credit); results already recorded stay visible if it is turned off again. The score and its band appear as soon as every question is answered and are saved with the answers (encrypted). For AUDIT-C, choose the cut-off (men 4+, women 3+; unspecified uses 3+). Give the same measure again over time: the tab and the Overview show the trend. Click a result to see the answers; **Delete** is for a questionnaire entered in error.
* **PHQ-9 question 9.** Any answer above "Not at all" raises a **safety alert** when the PHQ-9 is saved, whatever the total: an urgent to-do for today is put on your list, and the alert offers the client's signed safety plan (or **Write a safety plan now**, which opens a note in the *Safety plan* format). Follow your program's crisis protocol; for imminent danger call 988 or 911.
* **Program outcomes** are on **Reports → Outcome measures**: for each measure, how many clients were screened, how many were screened twice or more in the period, the mean first and last scores, and the number and percentage who improved (lower is better for PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C and DAST-10; higher for wellbeing). **Export (de-identified CSV)** gives one row per client and measure, keyed by a record id drawn at random for that file (not the client code), with the year rather than dates and no answers or names.
* Instruments not included: BAM, BARC-10 and other questionnaires whose licence for use in software could not be confirmed. A program that holds a licence can set one up as a county form (**Forms**).
* SUDS does **not** do billing, claims, e-prescribing (eRx) or medication administration records (eMAR).

## Referrals and resources
The **Resource Directory** holds treatment providers, MAT clinics, shelters, harm reduction, legal aid, transport, etc. Keep *Last verified* current (entries older than 6 months are flagged). The **Referrals we make** page (and the client's Referrals tab) holds the referrals the program makes to other providers — who referred a client *to* the program is on their intake. Create a **referral** (**+ Referral**), link the **consent** that authorizes sharing — it must name that provider (or its organization) — then update the status as it moves: pending → contacted → accepted/waitlisted → scheduled → admitted → completed. The outcome and any barrier are recorded when it closes (**Record outcome**), so a new referral asks for them only when it is entered as already over.

* **No consent names the provider?** Under *Consent*, **Record a consent naming …** turns the referral dialog to the consent form filled in for that provider — a Part 2 consent to disclose, the provider as recipient, *Referral and care coordination* as the purpose, and what the program's usual consent covers and when it expires. Check it against the signed form and save: you are back in the referral, as you left it, with the new consent chosen. **Back to the referral** (or Escape) returns without recording one.
* **Provider not listed?** *＋ Add a provider that is not on this list…* asks for its name and category (and a phone and city if you have them) in the same dialog, adds it to the directory and chooses it; finish its profile under Resources later.
* The **Referrals** list shows the client's name to anyone who can open the client (the code for everyone else). Its *Consent* column says **Consent on file** only when a live Part 2 consent names that provider; otherwise **No Part 2 consent** (a general release, or a consent naming someone else, does not count).

**Provider pictures.** After adding a starter directory, **Download provider pictures** replaces each program's generated card with the picture from the provider's own website: on an office server it downloads them (the server needs internet access), on SUDS on this device it uses the copies the site was published with. When it finishes it says how many pictures it got — on screen and in a pop-up message — and, under "Why some had no picture", why the rest keep their generated card. On a phone or tablet that syncs with an office server the button is not shown: the office's pictures arrive with the next sync. You can always add your own photos on a program's page (**+ Add pictures**).

**Pictures on a program's page.** A program can have up to 12 pictures; the first is its main picture and the directory card's cover. There are four ways to add one, and all of them save the picture the same way (shrunk on your device first):
* **+ Add pictures** opens your computer's or phone's file window. That window belongs to the operating system and can open behind the browser; the card says when one has opened.
* **Drag** picture files from a folder or another window onto the Pictures card (it is outlined while you drag), or **paste** a copied picture (Ctrl+V / ⌘V) anywhere on the program's page outside a text box.
* **Add from a web address** takes the address of a picture (https:// only), with an optional caption. On an office server the server downloads it — or, given the address of a web page, the preview picture that page advertises — with the same safety checks as provider pictures: never an address on the office network, JPEG, PNG or WebP only, 2 MB at most. The audit log records the site's name, not the full address. On SUDS on this device, and on a phone or tablet copy, your browser fetches the picture itself, and only from the site SUDS is served from (its Content-Security-Policy allows nothing else, and most websites would refuse anyway): otherwise you see *Pictures from other sites cannot be copied from this browser* — save the picture to the device and use **+ Add pictures**, or drag it onto the card. (On a device, a plain http:// address is accepted only for the site SUDS itself is served from, or for this computer.)

Do not add pictures of clients.

## Supervision
The **Supervision** page is the work waiting on a supervisor: notes to countersign, unsigned drafts across the team, staff time to approve, open client rights requests, and referrals with no outcome recorded.
* **Unsigned notes across your team** — each draft has **Open note** (the note opens over the page, as for countersigning) and **Remind author**, which gives its author a to-do on that client's record, due today (high priority when the draft is overdue), asking them to finish and sign it. The to-do says which kind of note and when it was started, nothing from the note itself; like every to-do its title and details are encrypted, and it is in the audit log. Once a reminder is open the row shows **Sent** with its date instead of the button, whoever sent it; when the author closes the to-do, a new one can be sent. You are never offered a reminder for your own draft.
* **Remind all overdue authors** sends one reminder per overdue draft after you confirm; the confirmation says how many it will send and how many are skipped because a reminder for that note is still open. Pressing it again sends nothing new.

## Notes
* **Administrative / contact notes** — visible to the whole care team; use for contacts, coordination, logistics.
* **Clinical notes** — only clinicians and supervisors can write or read them; use SOAP/DAP/BIRP/GIRP formats (sections auto-build the narrative).
* Notes are saved as **drafts**. **Sign & lock** (re-enter your password) when complete. Signed notes cannot be edited or deleted; add an **addendum** for corrections or late entries.
* **Verify signature** on a signed note checks the note as it is stored now against what was signed: *Signature intact* means it is exactly as signed; *Changed after signing* means the stored note no longer matches — report it to your privacy officer. Click the signature hash to see it in full.
* Do not put names or other identifiers in intervention *summary* fields — they are not encrypted. Use notes for anything sensitive.

## Consents (42 CFR Part 2)
Your program is a **42 CFR Part 2** program: the client header says so, and the rules below are enforced — SUDS refuses what the rule does not allow and tells you why.

* **Give the patient notice.** When a client starts, give them the program's notice of privacy practices (Privacy & Part 2 → Patient notice has the current version to print) and record **+ Notice given** on their Consents tab. Until you do, the Overview says *Not recorded* and Home counts them.
* **Record the consent.** **+ Consent** starts on the single consent for **treatment, payment and health care operations** (for a referral, use **Record a consent naming …** in the referral form instead), with the wording the rule allows. Tick what information it covers — the ticks are what SUDS enforces (an automated disclosure shares only those), and they are written into the consent as its scope; anything else the signed form says about what it covers goes in the optional note below them (kept, not enforced) — then when it expires, who signed (the client, or a parent, guardian or representative — then their name), and tick each statement only if the signed form really contains it: the right to revoke in writing, the redisclosure statement, and what happens if the client refuses to sign. Use a separate consent for **SUD counseling notes** or for use in a **legal proceeding** — each of those covers nothing else. A general **ROI** is not a Part 2 consent. **Print** on a consent gives a PDF of everything recorded. A consent marked *Pre-2024 form* is still in force; renew it on the current form when you next see the client.
* **A consent covers only who it names.** A referral, a disclosure or a file under a consent is refused unless the consent names the recipient (case, spacing and punctuation do not matter; a referral is checked against the provider's name and its organisation). On the treatment, payment and operations consent, write the providers' names, e.g. *County Behavioral Health, Riverbend OTP and my other treating providers*: the class wording on its own names nobody SUDS can check. If a consent really does cover a recipient SUDS cannot match, a supervisor can override, with a written reason that is kept with the disclosure.
* **Record every disclosure.** Referrals record their own. For anything else shared, **+ Disclosure**: who, what, why, how and on what basis. When you share under a consent, SUDS shows the **§2.32 notice** — put it on the fax cover, letter or email. Tick *includes SUD counseling notes* or *for use in a legal proceeding* when that is what it is; SUDS then asks for the consent or court order that covers it.
* **Without consent.** A referral can rest only on the client's consent, a medical emergency (with a written reason), a court order, or a supervisor's justified override. On **+ Disclosure**: a disclosure to a qualified service organization, or for research or an audit, needs the agreement or approval with that organization on file (Privacy & Part 2 → Agreements); research, audits, reports of a crime on the premises and child-abuse reports are recorded by a supervisor, with a written reason for the last two.
* A consent marked *Cannot authorise a disclosure* is missing a required element (for example one that came from a device without them): record a new one.
* **Courts and subpoenas.** A subpoena alone never allows you to share a client's record. A supervisor records the **court order** on the Consents tab (Court orders); only an order with the required findings and notice can be relied on. Ask your supervisor and county counsel before responding to any legal request.
* **Agreed restrictions.** If a client's restriction request has been agreed (Requests tab), SUDS asks you to confirm you have checked it before anything is shared.
* **Revoke** a consent when the client withdraws it: open referrals that relied on it are flagged.

## Privacy & Part 2 (supervisors and administrators)
The **Privacy & Part 2** page (sidebar) has the program-level controls; navigators and clinicians see the patient notice and the list of their clients who have not been given it.
* **Overview** — counts of what needs attention; an administrator sets whether this is a Part 2 program and the size of identified export that opens a draft incident. Switching Part 2 off needs a written reason; it is audited, shown to administrators on Home until switched back on, and opens a draft incident for the privacy officer.
* **Agreements** — qualified service organization agreements, and the IRB or oversight approvals for research and audits, with every name the organization goes by. Disclosures on those bases may only go to an organization registered here. Supervisors and administrators add and end them; everyone who records disclosures can see them.
* **Patient notice** — the notice of privacy practices, ready to print. An administrator edits it (each save is a new version) once counsel has approved the wording.
* **Complaints** — log every privacy complaint, named or anonymous. Record whether the person was told they may also complain to HHS, check no adverse action followed, and close it with its resolution. Complaints are never deleted.
* **Incidents & breaches** — record anything that may have exposed client information the day it is discovered. Fill in the four-factor risk assessment, make the determination (a breach is presumed unless the assessment shows a low probability of compromise), link the affected clients and record each notice as it goes out (a client whose record is later purged stays listed by code, as breach documentation). The deadline (60 days from discovery) and whether HHS and the media must be told are worked out for you; Home warns two weeks before and when late. A failed audit-log check, a break-glass access you flag as a concern (Supervision → Access to review → *Flag a concern*) and a very large identified export open a draft here automatically.

## Staff accounts and individual permissions (administrators)
**Settings → Users & permissions** lists every account. **Edit** holds the account itself (name, role, supervisor, password reset, and so on). Each row also has a **Permissions** button, and a badge such as *2 overrides* when the person has permissions granted or denied individually on top of their role (*Role only* when they have none). **Permissions** opens a dialog of its own: the role's baseline, the effective permissions with where each comes from (*role*, *granted*, *denied*), the individual overrides with their reasons, and the form to grant or deny one — every change needs a reason of at least 10 characters, is in the audit log, and a deny always wins. When you create an account, its Permissions dialog opens as soon as you have noted the temporary password, so an override is set in the same visit; close it (**Done**) to finish. ## Changing the choices on forms (administrators)
**Settings → Lists** holds the drop-down choices on documentation forms, grouped by form and named the way the forms name them: *What did you do?*, Location, Modality and Outcome on visits; *Who* and the call and text outcomes; referral *Status / What happened* and *If it did not happen, why*; the overdose form's *What happened* and *Given by*; time categories; note formats; primary substance; reasons for discharge. An administrator (the `settings:manage` permission) can, for each list:

* **Reword a choice.** Type the new wording and press Save (or Enter). Forms, lists, filters, reports and Excel/CSV exports all use it. Only the wording changes: records keep the same stored value, so old records and reports mean what they always did. Clear the box and save to go back to the built-in wording.
* **Reorder** with the ↑ and ↓ buttons.
* **Hide (retire) a choice** the program does not use. It is no longer offered on new records; records that already have it still show it, and editing one keeps it (shown as *no longer offered*). **Show** brings it back.
* **Add a choice** of your own. SUDS makes its stored value from the wording, and never one that a built-in choice already uses. A choice you added can be reworded or hidden like any other.
* **Restore defaults**: the built-in wording and order, with every built-in choice offered again. Choices you added are hidden, not deleted, because records may use them.

Choices marked **Used by SUDS** drive a count or an automatic step — for example a call *reached* or a text *replied* counts as contact with the client, a *fatal* overdose or a *deceased* discharge marks the client deceased, outreach and naloxone distribution can be recorded without a client — so they can be reworded but not hidden. Referral statuses and the overdose *What happened* choices can be reworded and reordered but not added to, because each one means something to the consent check and the counts. Race and ethnicity, ASAM level, stage of change, consent types, patient-rights requests and funding types are national or legal code sets and are not editable; the page lists them and says why.

An administrator sees a small **Edit this list** link beside each of these drop-downs on the forms themselves, which opens that list. Staff see changes the next time they sign in or reload. The lists belong to the office SUDS: a device that syncs with it receives them and cannot change them; SUDS on this device (with no office) keeps its own.

**Funding sources** are at the bottom of the same tab for anyone who manages the budget (`budget:manage`: administrators, supervisors, finance — finance uses **Funding & spending**, which has the same controls): add one quickly (name, type, period and total award; budget lines and the rest are on Funding & spending), rename it, or deactivate it so it is no longer offered on new records (records already charged to it keep it). A **Manage** link beside every *Funding source* drop-down opens it.

## Reports and Excel
Choose a date range for program summaries and monthly trends. The dates are calendar days where the program is (**Settings → Program → Your program → Organisation time zone**, else the server's `ORG_TIMEZONE`; SUDS on this device starts with the browser's time zone), so an evening visit on the last day of a quarter is in that quarter. In the funder report, *people served* is everyone with a visit or a call in the period (only visits charged to it, when a funding source is chosen), and every per-person count and breakdown on the page is counted within that same group. **Episodes of care** shows admissions in the period (and how many are still open), discharges in the period by reason, and the list of admissions; a client code opens the client only for roles that can see client records. **Export to Excel** (or CSV) is available on Reports, in three groups: *Funder & program* (everything as one workbook — which also works in local mode — the funder report, staff time, resources and, for roles that see the budget, funding, budget lines and expenditures), *De-identified records* (clients, visits, calls, referrals, to-dos, consents, episodes, overdose events, client forms and disclosures) and *Records (identified)*; exports are also on the Clients, Resources, Visits, Calls, Time, Referrals and Budget pages. De-identified exports (the default) carry no names and no client codes: each row has a record id drawn at random for that file, dates are reduced to the year, and free text is left out. Supervisors can produce an identified workbook, which is recorded in the audit log.

## Funder report, funding attribution and harm-reduction reports
**Funder report.** Counts people, each once. The first thing on the page says what kind of report it is. A supervisor, an administrator or **finance** (the person who usually writes the grant report; finance sees the counts, never a client record) opens it as **Submission to your funder — not for publication**, for any range or one funding source: exact counts, every figure the true number, which is what a funder asks for at quarter end (choose **Counts → Small cells suppressed** if you would rather hide small groups; the file says which). Publishing or sharing figures beyond the funder is a separate step, **Prepare a publication release** (below). Two plain lines under the heading say how the figures were counted; **Why some numbers are hidden** (or **About these counts**) opens the full statement, which is also on the file's About sheet. In a suppressed report or a publication release, every count of people with fewer than the small-cell threshold (11; **Settings → Program → Reporting**) shows as `<11` so nobody in a small group can be picked out — breakdown rows (race, gender, language…), people per fund, overdose events and reversals by month, discharge reasons, and a total that is itself small — and wherever a hidden figure could still be worked out from the other figures in the report, more is hidden and shows as `suppressed` (the total too, only when nothing else will do; a breakdown too small to show safely is withheld). Kits, test strips, services, hours and money are exact; naloxone doses are exact unless they would show how many reversals a hidden cell holds. In a publication release, funds that served fewer people than the threshold are listed together in one row, **Other funds (*n* combined)**, with how many funds it holds and their staff hours; its people and services show as `withheld`. The NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page count the same way. In a publication release every month of the period and every "given by" and discharge reason is listed, even when it is 0, rare typed-in values (a language only a few people speak) beyond the first ten are combined as *Other (combined)*, and a table whose protection SUDS cannot confirm is **withheld** and listed with the reason, while the rest is published: publish each period once, never a period that overlaps or contains another you have published, and look over what is withheld before you share it. People served is never hidden while new admissions or episodes opened are shown: they are hidden with it. Small cells are screened automatically, which is not a guarantee: before a publication release's file downloads you tick **I have reviewed the withheld and small figures before sharing** (recorded in the audit log). SUDS checks that the pattern of what it hides says nothing more to someone who knows how SUDS works; a period is refused only when even the number of people served cannot be shown safely, which happens for a small program's month now and then (publish the quarter or the year instead) and does not affect the submission to your funder.

**Publishing.** Only one kind of run is a **publication release**: all funding sources, for one calendar month, quarter or fiscal year (starting in January, April, July or October) that has **ended**. A supervisor or an administrator prepares one under **Prepare a publication release** (for the range on screen when it is one, or *Last month*, *Last quarter* or a fiscal year); the page then lists what was withheld and why, what to check before publishing, and the review tick box, with **Back to the submission to your funder**. The heading then says *Publication release*, and the report and its files say *Publication release — small cells screened; review before sharing* (the filename ends `publication-screened-review-before-sharing`). Any other run — one funding source, a custom date range, a year to date, or a navigator's own caseload — is a submission to the funder or an internal report, **not for publication**, and so is its file (the filename ends `exact-counts` or `internal-suppressed`): two reports of different scope or period can be subtracted from each other to reveal a small group, however carefully each one is suppressed. Publish **one standard period per funder cycle** (for example the fiscal year, once its data are complete): a quarter and the year that contains it can still be subtracted from each other. **Who can run what.** Supervisors and administrators can run any report: a custom range, one funding source, a period not yet ended (all *internal, not for publication*), and exact counts. Navigators and clinicians run it for their own caseload, which is always internal and always suppressed. **Finance and read-only accounts run publication releases only** — the page says so, opens on the last quarter that has ended and offers only the *Periods you can publish*; a link to any other run says why it is refused. Any internal run could be subtracted from a release to reveal someone (August's release minus 1–30 August is whoever was served on the 31st), so internal runs stay with the roles that can already see client records or run the program. Finance's money and staff hours are exact in a publication release and on **Funding & spending** for any period or fund. The same rule applies to the NDP log and the opioid settlement report on the Reports page (the settlement report counts the whole program, so a navigator gets it only as a publication release too, and only while caseloads are not restricted). The program's **own submission to its funder** (a funder's own fund's figures, or the whole program's) is what a supervisor or administrator gets by default: every cell is the true number, the heading says *Submission to your funder — not for publication*, and the exported file says *Exact counts … not for publication or sharing* on its About sheet (a CSV says it in its first rows; the filename ends `exact-counts`). On the Reports page, **Kind of file** beside the NDP log and the settlement report offers the same three: the submission (exact), the submission with small cells suppressed, and a publication release (for a range that can be one). **This report (Excel)** / **CSV** download the report as shown. On the funder report they are under **Which file do I send?**, each with a line saying what it is for: **This report (Excel)** is the submission workbook to send your funder (for a publication release, the file to publish or share once reviewed); **CSV** is the same figures as plain rows, for a funder portal or data system that asks for an upload; **Export everything to Excel** is every program sheet for the period, for your own checking — not for sending.

**Funding source on visits.** A new visit is pre-filled with the worker's default fund (**Settings → Users & permissions → Edit → Default funding source**) or, if they have none, the program's (**Settings → Program → Reporting**); the worker can still choose another or none. A visit logged by someone who is not shown the field (a clinician) is charged to the same default. The funder report's **By funding source** table has a **No funding source** row, and a warning with a link to exactly those visits so each can be edited. Staff hours: **Approved staff hours** is what a county would invoice; **Logged, not yet approved** (draft or submitted time) is shown beside it, with a warning, so time waiting for approval does not read as zero.

**Harm-reduction reporting** (Reports, for the date range chosen). See [docs/compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md](compliance/HARM-REDUCTION-REPORTING.md) for every field and what is not yet verified.
* **Naloxone distribution & reversal log (NDP-style)**, Excel or CSV: one row per day, site type and recipient type with kits and doses distributed (doses = kits × *Naloxone doses per kit*, 2 unless changed in Settings), and one row per day, site type and who gave it for reversals reported (overdose events where naloxone was used and the person survived). Kits come from *Naloxone kits given* on visits, including community distribution with no client; the site type is the visit's *Location*. No names or client codes. The layout follows the NDP's reporting fields as SUDS understands them and **is not the official NDP template**: check it against the current template before submitting.
* **Opioid settlement expenditures**, Excel or CSV (budget roles): spending from opioid settlement funds grouped by allowable use (the settlements' Exhibit E core strategies and approved uses) and California High Impact Abatement Activity, with the share that went to High Impact Abatement Activities, pending spending shown apart, and services charged to those funds. Set the category on the fund (**Funding & spending → Edit**; the setup wizard asks it for a main fund that is opioid settlement money), and on an expenditure only when it differs. The category lists need verification against each fund's agreement. When no fund is settlement money, the Reports page says so beside the buttons, and the file carries a row saying so rather than a bare header.

**Publication releases switched off.** An administrator can switch publication releases off (**Settings → Program → Modules → Publication releases**) while the program waits for its own review of the method. Then *Prepare a publication release* disappears, the submission to your funder carries on as before, and a read-only account is told it has nothing to run until releases are switched back on.

**Settlement spending for DHCS or your county.** Beside the settlement report, **DHCS settlement expenditure layout** gives the same spending one row per activity in the fields of DHCS's expenditure reporting form, with the categories, amounts and people served filled in and the narrative columns (which settlement, the activity's name and description, why it meets the HIAA, outcomes) left for you to write; numbers of people of 10 or fewer are written "10 or fewer", as DHCS asks. **Set up the county template** (finance, supervisors, administrators) matches your county's own form: name it and list its columns in order, each filled from one of those fields, blank, or fixed text; **County template** then downloads the rows in the county's columns. Check both against the current forms.

## SUPRT-A (State Opioid Response client-level reporting)
Shown when the program has a **SOR grant** funding source (or an administrator switches the module on). A client who receives a service charged to the SOR grant needs a **baseline**; then a **reassessment** at 6 months (3 if your contract says so), an **annual assessment** each year, and a **closeout** when services end. Each reassessment and annual assessment is collected in a window from 30 days before to 30 days after the anniversary. The client's **SUPRT-A** tab shows what is due and opens the form: what SUDS already knows (the client code, substance, MAT, overdoses, screenings, diagnoses, services since the last assessment, demographics) is filled in and says so; change anything that is not right and ask the rest. Record whether the client completed SAMHSA's own questionnaire (SUPRT-C), which SUDS does not hold. Follow-ups due are on your **To-dos** page (a supervisor sees the team's). The **SUPRT-A** page (Reports) shows completion rates and, for a supervisor, makes the **file for entry into SPARS**: it names clients, so it asks to whom and on what basis, leaves out anyone without a consent naming the recipient, and is written to each client's accounting of disclosures. Check it against the current SUPRT handbook before entering it; see [docs/compliance/SUPRT.md](compliance/SUPRT.md).

## State reporting (CalOMS Tx) and the county EHR hand-off
**Reports → State reporting** (shown when the program uses the CalOMS Tx or county EHR hand-off module). A treatment program that must report CalOMS Tx has an administrator turn it on there (tick *This program reports CalOMS Tx* and enter the program's CalOMS provider ID). From then on, **Start an episode** on a client's Episodes tab asks the CalOMS admission questions, and **Discharge** asks the CalOMS discharge status and date of last service (and the past-30-day questions, unless the discharge is administrative). The **CalOMS records** button on each episode shows what it has, any problems, and what is still needed — for example an admission record for an episode opened at intake, or an annual update on each anniversary of admission. Prevention and navigation programs leave it off and are never asked.

The **Validation report** lists every problem in the period by client code and field (fatal errors first); fix them from the client's Episodes tab. A supervisor can **Download preview** — a zip of CSV files plus the monthly provider activity report, marked *PREVIEW - NOT FOR SUBMISSION*, with no names or dates of birth — to check it. When the records are ready, **Produce submission file**: that is the disclosure, so each client in it gets a *State reporting (CalOMS)* entry in their accounting of disclosures and its records show as sent, and the file downloads. Send that file, unchanged, through the county's CalOMS process; it is listed under *Submissions produced* (with its SHA-256) and can be downloaded again for 90 days. Records that still have fatal errors are held back. The layout is not yet verified against the current DHCS data dictionary: read `docs/compliance/CALOMS.md` before the first submission.

**SUDS does not submit Drug Medi-Cal claims.** Where a service must be billed, the **County EHR hand-off** on the same page produces the encounters (one row per client, per day, per kind of service and worker, with minutes, place and funding source) for entry into the county EHR. It includes names and Medi-Cal IDs, so it needs a recipient, a purpose and a lawful basis; with the consent basis, clients with no consent on file naming that recipient are left out and listed. It is never made on an "other" basis: a supervisor's justified override covers one client's disclosure, not a whole file. Identified exports on Reports work the same way; an *internal* export may only go to this program or its staff, and a QSOA, research or audit export needs the agreement or approval with the recipient on file. See `docs/SCOPE.md`.

## Working faster
* **Keyboard shortcuts.** **/** puts the cursor in the search box at the top, **n** opens *Log a visit*, **?** lists the shortcuts, and **Ctrl + Enter** (**⌘ + Enter** on a Mac) saves the form that is open, exactly as its Save button would. The single keys work only when you are not typing in a field and no dialog is open. If they get in your way (for example with speech recognition, which can send a single letter), switch them off under **My profile → Keyboard shortcuts**; Ctrl + Enter keeps working. This is the setting WCAG 2.1.4 asks for.
* **Undo.** Deactivating a program in the resource directory, retiring a policy or contract, and ending someone's assignment to a client's care team happen at once, with an **Undo** toast for 10 seconds (it stays while the pointer is over it or its button has the keyboard, and it is read out by screen readers). Undo puts the program back in the pickers, the document back in effect, or the same worker back on the case in the same role (a new assignment from today; the ended one stays in the history). Both the action and the undo are in the audit log. Anything that cannot be taken back — deleting a record, signing a note, deactivating a staff account — still asks first.
* **Nothing here yet?** An empty list says what to do next, with the button that does it when you may; when you may not, it says who does (for example: "An administrator or supervisor uploads the program's policies and contracts").
* **The ? beside a heading** explains it. The Part 2 consent form's **Is this consent a valid basis for sharing?** says which consent covers what (TPO, a single recipient, counseling notes, legal proceedings) and what never counts (a general release, a verbal OK, one that has expired or been revoked). **Adjust stock**, **Dispose of** and **Move stock** on Supplies each say when to use which: dispose of stock you are throwing away, move stock between your sites, adjust only when the count is wrong — and never adjust for kits handed out (log the visit). The funder report's **Which file do I send?** says which download goes to the funder.
